Web design in Knoxville, and why local context matters

Knoxville runs on the University of Tennessee, Oak Ridge National Laboratory nearby, healthcare and outdoor tourism heading into the Smokies. That mix decides who your customers are and how they look for you, and it is why a website built for Knoxville should not read like one built for anywhere else. The independent businesses here cluster around Market Square, the Old City and Bearden — and the ones doing well are usually the ones easiest to find and quickest to answer.

Who we build for in Knoxville

Most of the work we do in Knoxville is for restaurants, trades, professional services and outfitters serving Smokies traffic. Different trades, same problem: the phone rings while you are busy, the enquiry arrives at nine at night, and the customer who could not reach you calls the next name on the list. A website will not fix a staffing problem, but it will stop the ones who never called from disappearing silently.
Football weekends change the whole city. Businesses that plan for those seven Saturdays do noticeably better than those who treat them as normal. A site that can be updated for a weekend rush is worth more than one nobody can touch without calling a developer.

Getting found in Knoxville

There is no trick to appearing in Knoxville searches. Google wants to know where you are, what you do, which areas you cover and whether anyone vouches for you. Most local business sites answer one of those four. We answer all of them as part of building the thing, because leaving it out and charging for it later is a poor way to treat a customer.

How the build works

The order of work is deliberately backwards from how most agencies do it. We build the site first — your photos, your words, your real hours. You look at a finished site for your business, not a mockup with your logo dropped into a template. If it is wrong, we change it. You pay when you are happy, and not before.
A site that looks impressive on a big monitor and falls apart on a phone is worse than no site at all, because it costs you the customer while looking like it is working. We build for the small screen first and let the desktop version follow.
