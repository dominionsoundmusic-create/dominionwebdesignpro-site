Web design in Savannah, and why local context matters

Savannah runs on the port, tourism, SCAD and a historic district that shapes what every local business looks like. That mix decides who your customers are and how they look for you, and it is why a website built for Savannah should not read like one built for anywhere else. The independent businesses here cluster around Broughton Street, the Starland District and the squares of the historic downtown — and the ones doing well are usually the ones easiest to find and quickest to answer.

Who we build for in Savannah

Most of the work we do in Savannah is for restaurants, tour operators, galleries, trades working on historic properties and logistics firms serving the port. Different trades, same problem: the phone rings while you are busy, the enquiry arrives at nine at night, and the customer who could not reach you calls the next name on the list. A website will not fix a staffing problem, but it will stop the ones who never called from disappearing silently.
With an art and design college in the middle of town, the visual standard customers are used to is high. A cheap-looking site reads as out of place. Design credibility is worth more in Savannah than in most cities its size.

Getting found in Savannah

Local search is won on unglamorous things. In Savannah the businesses that come up first are rarely the ones with the prettiest sites — they are the ones whose details are consistent everywhere, whose pages say plainly which areas they cover, and who have recent reviews. None of that is expensive. It is just routinely skipped.

How the build works

We build before we bill. That means the risk of the thing being wrong sits with us rather than with you, which is the right way round — you are the one running a business in Savannah with better things to do than manage a web project.
Almost everyone who finds you in Savannah will do it on a phone, often outdoors, often on a signal that is not great. Every site we build is designed for that first and checked on a desktop afterwards — the opposite of how most sites are made, and the reason so many local business sites are slow and cramped.
