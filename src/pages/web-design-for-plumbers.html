Why web design for plumbers is its own kind of job

Web design for plumbers starts with an uncomfortable fact: a lot of your best customers find you while something is going wrong. Water on the floor, no hot water before work, a sewer smell in the hall bath. They are not browsing. They search on a phone, open two or three plumbing websites, and call the first one that clearly fixes their problem in their town and can be reached right now.

That changes how the site is built. The phone number is not tucked into a footer. The emergency line, if you run one, is stated in plain words with real hours. Every service gets its own page, because "water heater repair" and "slab leak detection" are different searches from different people. And the license number your state expects is on the page, because careful homeowners look for it.

Dominion Web Design Pro builds these sites one at a time. Maurice Johnson writes every page by hand for your shop and your service area, and you get to look at a free demo before deciding anything.

Two different plumbing customers, two different pages

Most plumbing sites treat every visitor the same. They are not. The person with a burst supply line wants one thing: proof you can come today. The person planning to replace a 15-year-old water heater, or repipe an older house, has time to read, compare and ask about price.

The plumbing website checklist

Every plumber site we build is checked against this list before it goes live.

  • Tap-to-call number in the header of every page, readable at arm's length on a phone
  • Emergency availability stated honestly: if you do not run nights, the site does not say 24/7
  • A page for each main service, written for that job rather than a list of bullet points
  • The towns and neighborhoods you will drive to, on every page and on their own pages where it makes sense
  • Your license number shown where your state's advertising rules call for it
  • Real photos of your trucks, crew and work, not stock images of models holding wrenches
  • A short contact form that emails each lead straight to you
  • Hours, a Google map of your service area and links to your Google Business Profile and Facebook page
  • Page titles, descriptions, schema and a sitemap submitted to Google
  • Fast load on a cell signal, with photos compressed and sized for the screen

License numbers on a plumbing website in Texas

Plumbing is a licensed trade in Texas, and the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners oversees it. The board's rules say the Responsible Master Plumber of record and the owner of a plumbing company must make sure advertisements meant to get plumbing business show the Responsible Master Plumber's license number, regardless of the type of media used. Business cards, flyers and handouts are named as examples. A website that advertises your services is advertising, so we put the number where people will see it, usually in the header or footer and again on the about page.

Customers can check that number themselves. The board runs a public license search that shows whether a license is current, when it expires, and any disciplinary actions, and the board's consumer page tells homeowners to look for the license number on written estimates and invoices. A site that shows the number openly saves a careful customer a step and tells them you have nothing to hide.

Outside Texas, the rules are set by your own state, and sometimes your city. Tell Maurice what your licensing board requires and the site will show it. We do not give legal advice on licensing; your board is the authority.

Plain note: we only put on the site the license and registration numbers you give us, copied exactly as issued. Since anyone can look a number up on the board's search, it has to be right.

Your service area, Google and the address question

Many plumbers work out of a truck and a home office. Google's guidelines for Business Profiles say a service-area business should hide its address from customers, and they use a plumber working from a home address as the example. You list the area you serve instead, and Google asks that it not reach much past about two hours of driving from where you are based.

The website should match. It names the towns you cover, not your street. On the Pro package, Maurice sets up your Google Business Profile and writes ten city pages for the towns you want more calls from, each researched for that town rather than copied with the name swapped.

Reviews and photos that sound like your shop

A homeowner choosing between two plumbers they have never met leans on two things: what other customers say and what the work looks like. You control the second completely. Snap the new water heater strapped and vented, the clean trench after a sewer repair, the cabinet under the sink with new supply lines. Those pictures on a service page say more than a paragraph of claims.

Reviews you earn, and then show. On Pro and Agency, your real Google reviews display on the site automatically. We never write reviews, and the site never puts a star rating on a page that the customers did not give you. The best time to ask for one is when the customer has hot water again and is still standing in the kitchen.
