What affordable website design for small business should mean

Affordable website design for small business should mean a fair, fixed price for a site that does its job, not the lowest possible number with the real costs hidden further down the road. Our lowest price is $497, paid once, for a five-page site written by hand. That is a real website. It is also a small one, and this page is about being clear on the difference.

Owners looking for an inexpensive website often run into two extremes. One is a few thousand dollars from an agency. The other is "free" from a builder that turns into a subscription. Neither is wrong for everyone. The point of this page is to show where a low one-time price sits between them and what you give up to get it.

The monthly builder trap, in plain numbers

Subscription builders advertise a low monthly price, and the price is real. In October 2026 Wix's Light plan is listed at about $17 a month and Squarespace's Basic plan at about $19 a month, both when billed for a year up front, with month-to-month billing costing more. Squarespace raised its prices in July 2026, which is a good reminder that these numbers change. Check the builders' own pricing pages before you decide.

The trap is not the fee. It is three things that come with it:

  1. It never endsStop paying and the site goes offline. Over five years, the plans above add up to roughly $1,000 to $1,150, more if you move up a tier along the way.
  2. You rent the site, not own itThe design and pages live inside that platform. Wix and Squarespace both say in their own help centers that a site's design cannot be moved to another host, so leaving usually means rebuilding.
  3. The writing is still your jobThe builder gives you a layout with sample text. Turning that into clear pages about your services and your towns is the hard part, and it is the part that gets left half done.

To be fair about our side: hosting here is $49 a month if you do not keep the AI agent, which is more than the cheapest builder plan. What the difference pays for is a finished, hand-written site that belongs to you, plus someone to call when something on it needs changing. If you have the time and like doing it yourself, a builder may well be the better deal. The website builder versus web designer guide goes through that choice in detail.

Honest tradeoffs of a low-cost site

A low cost web design service saves money by doing less, so the question is what it does less of. Some cut the writing and fill pages with generic copy. Some cut testing, so forms break and phone numbers do not tap. Some cut ownership, keeping the domain in their name so leaving costs you.

We cut size instead. Starter is five pages, not fifteen. It uses a clean layout rather than a custom design. It covers your home town, not every town around it. Those are limits you can see and plan around before you buy.

What does not get cut at any price: hand-written words, a working form, a site that reads well on a phone, and a domain registered to you.

Ways to keep the cost down without cutting corners

The cheapest website is the one that only has to be built once. A few things on your side make that far more likely, whoever builds it.

  • Start with the size you need today. If nearly all your work is in one town, a five-page site is enough. Pages can be added later; paying now for pages you will not use is the opposite of affordable.
  • Gather your own photos first. Phone pictures of your truck, your counter, your finished work and your team at work beat stock images, and they cost nothing.
  • Write down your services and towns. A plain list of what you do, what you do not do and where you go saves rounds of back and forth.
  • Register the domain yourself. Do it in the business's name at a registrar you can log into. It is a small yearly cost and it keeps you free to change designers.
  • Grow in steps. More pages can come later, once the business reaches more towns and has more to say. Ask about it when the time comes.

None of this needs technical skill. It is the same homework a good designer would ask for anyway, and having it ready is the surest way to get a low-cost site live in the 7 to 14 days Starter allows.
