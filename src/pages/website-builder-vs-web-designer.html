How much does a website builder cost? For a small business site in October 2026, the main builders charge roughly $10 to $40 a month on an annual plan, and roughly a quarter to two fifths more if you pay month to month. Online store plans start near $29 a month and climb from there. That is the honest short answer. The longer answer is that the monthly fee is the smallest part of what a builder site really costs you, and the part people forget is their own time.

This guide lays out the actual plan prices for the four builders small businesses ask about most, what those prices leave out, a three-year cost comparison against hiring a designer (including us, with every recurring fee shown), and a plain way to decide. We do not earn anything if you pick a builder, and we will tell you when a builder is the better call.

What the monthly price leaves out

The plan fee buys the software and the hosting. It does not buy a finished website.

Things you still pay for

  • Your domain name after any free first year (Squarespace includes one eligible domain for the first year on annual website plans).
  • Professional email at your domain, usually a separate subscription.
  • Apps and add-ons for booking, reviews or extra forms, often a few dollars a month each.
  • Card processing on every online sale.
  • Paid photos if your own do not hold up on a large screen.

Things you still do yourself

  • Write every page: services, about, service area, contact.
  • Set page titles, descriptions and headings so Google knows what each page is for.
  • Connect the domain, the contact form and your Google Business Profile.
  • Check the site on phones, fix what breaks, and keep the hours and prices current.
  • Decide which towns and services deserve their own page, then write those too.

The builders are good software. The templates look clean and the hosting is solid. The hard part of a small business website was never the software. It is knowing what to say on each page, in what order, for the customer who is comparing you with two competitors on a phone in a parking lot.

When a website builder is the right choice

We would rather lose a sale than sell someone a site they do not need. A builder is the better answer when:

  • You are testing an idea. A side business that may not exist in six months does not need a designer. A $17 plan and a weekend will tell you more than a brochure will.
  • You like writing and you have the evenings. If you can describe your services clearly and you enjoy tinkering, a builder template will carry you a long way.
  • You sell products online first and foremost. Shopify is built for that job, with inventory, shipping and checkout handled. Our packages build sites for local service businesses, not online stores, so for a product catalog Shopify or Squarespace Commerce is the honest recommendation.
  • You need one page with your hours and a phone number. If that is genuinely all, a builder or even a complete Google Business Profile covers it.
  • The budget is the budget. If $497 is not there right now, start on a builder. A plain, accurate site beats no site.

When paying a web designer is worth it

A designer earns the money when the website has to bring in calls from strangers, not just confirm your hours for people who already know you. That usually means a trade or service business in a town with competition: plumbers, roofers, law offices, therapists, salons. Those customers search "service plus town," compare three results, and call the one that looks like it knows their problem.

Getting there takes pages written for each service and each town you serve, titles and descriptions that match how people search, schema markup, and a Google Business Profile that points to the right page. None of that is hard on its own. Doing it well for ten or thirty pages, while running a business, is where most do-it-yourself sites stall at five thin pages.

It is also worth paying when you have been burned. If a builder site has sat half-finished for a year, the cheapest option has quietly become the most expensive one.

How to make a small business website yourself, if you go the builder route

If the builder is your answer, do it in this order. It saves the most rework.

  1. Write the pages before you pick a template.A home page, one page per main service, an about page, a service area page and a contact page. Write them in a plain document first. The template is easier to choose once you know how much you have to say.
  2. Buy your own domain in your own name.Use your business name or name plus town. Make sure you are the registrant, not a helper or a builder reseller account you cannot reach later.
  3. Pick the cheapest paid plan that does the job.Most service businesses do not need a commerce tier. Start low; upgrading later is easy.
  4. Set a title and description on every page.Each one should say the service and the town in plain words, the way a customer would type it.
  5. Put the phone number at the top and make it tappable.Most local visitors are on a phone and want to call, not read.
  6. Connect your Google Business Profile.Add the website address to the profile and make sure the name, address and phone match the site exactly.
  7. Check it on your own phone, then ask someone else to.Fix anything that is hard to tap or read. Run it through our free Site Check for the search basics.
