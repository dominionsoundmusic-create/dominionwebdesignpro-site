A shopping city whose customers cross a river

McAllen sells far more than its own residents could buy. The Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas reported retail sales of $17.5 billion for McAllen in 2023, the highest of any Texas border metro. Its profile of the area describes retail tourism that draws customers from as far as Monterrey, about 150 miles away. The city's own sales tax take reached a record $96.8 million in 2024, which local officials credited to the two-country economy.

That share is shrinking, though. A January 2026 Dallas Fed study found Mexican shoppers make up a smaller part of Texas border retail than before the 2008 financial crisis, pointing to big-box stores opening in northern Mexico and longer waits at the ports of entry. For a McAllen shop that means the trip has to be worth it, and the website is where a shopper decides.

McAllen web design that treats the Valley as one market

People here cross city lines all day without thinking about it. Federal statistics group McAllen, Edinburg and Mission into one metro area, which was the fifth most populous in Texas at the 2020 count. Pharr, Weslaco, Donna, Alamo and Hidalgo are all a short drive away. A customer in Edinburg may never type "McAllen" into a search bar, and a plumber based in Pharr may do most of his work in McAllen.

Google ranks local results by relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot move your shop closer to every customer, but you can be clear. A retailer people drive to needs a page that makes the trip easy: address, parking, hours on weekends and holidays, and what is in stock. A service company that goes to the customer needs to name the cities it covers and say something true about each one. Those are two different sites, and we ask which one you are before writing a word.

Two languages, handled honestly

Census Bureau survey figures for 2019 to 2023 show about 73.6% of McAllen residents age five and older speak a language other than English at home. Plenty of customers read both, switch mid-search, or prefer one for certain things. Here is how we plan a site for that without pretending to be something we are not.

  1. Start with the English siteMaurice writes every English page by hand for your business, using your answers about what customers ask most.
  2. Decide which pages need SpanishUsually the home page, services and contact. Not every page needs a twin, and a smaller set of well-written pages beats a full set of rough ones.
  3. Spanish text comes from a fluent writer you trustYou, a relative, a staff member or a paid translator. Dominion does not write Spanish copy, so we will not fake it with a machine.
  4. We build those pages as real pagesEach with its own address and title, linked to its English version, so search engines and customers can find it directly.

Photos you already own beat stock pictures

A shopper deciding whether to drive across town wants to see the actual racks, the actual counter, the actual work. Your phone is good enough. Take a dozen pictures in good daylight: the storefront from the parking lot, the inside from the door, your best sellers, a finished job. We size them so they load fast on a phone. If you sell photography, quinceañera dresses or anything else people judge by eye, a gallery page is worth it, and Pro includes one.
