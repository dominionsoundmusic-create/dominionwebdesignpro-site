Web design in San Marcos, TX means building for customers who did not grow up here and may not stay long. Students arrive every August, river visitors come for a day, and I-35 brings a constant stream of travelers between Austin and San Antonio. None of them have a neighbor to ask. They search, and they choose from what they find. Dominion Web Design Pro builds a free demo of your site first, written for your business and those customers, so you can see it before you pay.

Three customer bases that keep turning over

Students. Texas State University reported a record fall 2025 enrollment of 44,596 students. A large share of them are new to town each year, and every one of them needs food, haircuts, phone repair, car service, apartments and a place to work out.

River visitors. The spring-fed San Marcos River draws swimmers, tubers and paddlers, most of whom plan their day on a phone, often on the drive in. They want parking, rental prices, hours and food nearby.

Highway traffic. San Marcos sits on I-35 between Austin and San Antonio, with outlet shopping and the frontage road businesses that serve people stopping for an hour.

In most towns, word of mouth slowly builds a business over years. In San Marcos the population changes too fast for that to work on its own. Your website and your Google listing are doing the work that a neighbor's recommendation does elsewhere.

Plan your web presence around the university calendar

Most owners treat the semester like the weather. It is closer to a schedule you can plan against.

  1. March to MayBuild or fix the website. Search engines need time to find and trust new pages.
  2. June and JulyFill in your Google Business Profile, post fresh photos and ask summer customers for reviews.
  3. August and SeptemberNew students search for everything in their first few weeks and keep much of what they find. Make sure your hours and offers are current.
  4. December and MayUpdate hours for breaks and graduation weekends, when the town empties and fills again.

Small business help in San Marcos

San Marcos has an advantage most towns its size do not: its own university hosts a Small Business Development Center. The Texas State University SBDC serves Hays and eleven other Central Texas counties, is part of the Texas South-West SBDC Network, works under a cooperative agreement with the U.S. Small Business Administration, and offers confidential counseling and training to small businesses at no or low cost.

The Greater San Marcos Partnership is the regional economic development organization for San Marcos and Hays and Caldwell counties. Its work includes helping existing local businesses grow, not only recruiting new ones. SCORE mentors also serve the area for free.

We are not connected to any of these groups. If you are starting out, talk to the SBDC before you spend money on anything, including a website.

What a new San Marcos business can skip

Owners starting out here are often sold more than they need. Some things are worth paying for later, once the business is steady. Some are rarely worth paying for at all.

  • A custom appA fast website that works on a phone does what most small businesses want an app for, at a fraction of the cost.
  • A huge page countA few strong pages beat dozens of thin ones. We do not sell page counts, and you should be wary of anyone who sells by the hundred.
  • A long contractOur website prices are one time. If someone wants twelve months locked in before you have seen a page, ask why.
  • Paid ads on day oneAds can work, but they send people to your site. Make sure the site is clear and your Google listing is complete before you pay for clicks.
  • A rebrand before you have customersA clean name, a simple logo and real photos carry a new business a long way. Refine the brand once you know who buys from you.

What you should not skip: a site that loads fast on a phone, your real hours, a working phone number and a contact form you have tested. Those four things decide more first visits in a college town than any logo ever will. Get them right, and the rest can wait until the business tells you what it needs.
