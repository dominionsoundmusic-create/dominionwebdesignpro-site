What the summer of 2022 taught Jackson businesses

The city went under a boil water notice on July 29, 2022. Late in August, flooding on the Pearl River pushed the O.B. Curtis treatment plant, already running on backup pumps, to the point of failure. On the night of August 29, Governor Tate Reeves declared a state of emergency, and the Mississippi National Guard was called in to help hand out water.

Restaurants felt it at once. The state hospitality and restaurant association said many of the roughly 500 restaurants in the area were having problems with the water. Owners drove to neighboring towns for ice, bought canned drinks and disposable plates, and one restaurant manager estimated as much as $2,500 a week in extra costs. At least one Fondren restaurant cited the water troubles when it closed for good that fall. In November 2022, a federal judge put an interim third-party manager in charge of the water system, which now runs as JXN Water.

Through all of it, customers had one question for every business: are you open, and is it safe? The owners who could answer it on their own website, quickly and clearly, spent less of the day on the phone.

Website design Jackson MS neighborhoods each need differently

Jackson is the state capital, and its customers sort themselves by neighborhood. A site that says only "Jackson" misses how people actually search and talk.

Fondren

North State Street is the main drag, lined with boutiques, galleries and neighborhood restaurants. On Fondren's First Thursday, a free evening that runs most months from spring into fall, shops and restaurants stay open late and spill onto the sidewalks while bands play. A Fondren shop's site should list that night's hours and specials.

Belhaven

A neighborhood inside the city, close to Fondren and downtown, known for its green spaces and a wide mix of architecture. Home service trades, tutors, therapists and small studios who work there do well with a page that names it.

Farish Street

Carved out in the 1870s and 1880s, Farish Street became the center of Black business life in Jackson under segregation, with lawyers, doctors, funeral homes, clubs and record labels such as Trumpet Records. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1980. Revival plans have stalled more than once, and anyone opening there is building on a long story worth telling on the about page.

Jackson State University

A public HBCU founded in 1877 as Natchez Seminary in Natchez, later moved to Jackson, on John R. Lynch Street. Student housing, food, printing, barbers and tutoring around campus all rise and fall with the academic calendar, and their sites should say so.

Across the county lines, Ridgeland and Madison sit north in Madison County, and Brandon and Flowood sit east in Rankin County. Each is its own town with its own searches. Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence, so a Flowood customer is more likely to see a business whose site actually talks about Flowood. A real page for each town you serve does that honestly. Nobody can promise a ranking, and we do not.
