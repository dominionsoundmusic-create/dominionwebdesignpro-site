Good Memphis website design gets one thing right that many sites get wrong: the hours. A lot of the people who need a diner, a tire shop, a gym, a child care provider or a tow truck are not looking at nine in the morning. They are looking at eleven at night or five in the morning, on a phone, after work or before it. If your site does not say clearly that you are open, and how to reach you right now, the search moves on to someone else.

Memphis website design for owners whose busy hours are after dark

Memphis has a real reason to be awake at night. FedEx runs its World Hub at Memphis International Airport, and in 2019 the company said the hub had about 11,000 team members and was the largest sort facility in the whole FedEx Express network. The main package sort happens overnight, with planes landing in a tight window after dark so packages can move on before morning. In October 2024 FedEx opened a new automated sort building at the hub, 1.3 million square feet over four levels, able to sort 56,000 packages an hour. Warehouses and trucking yards around the airport keep their own late schedules.

Think about who searches late. Someone coming off a shift wants a hot plate of food or a workout before bed. A parent working nights needs a sitter or a care center with early drop-off. A driver with a flat on the interstate needs a tire or a tow. None of them will dig through a menu called "Info" to find whether you are open.

Your site should put three things above the fold for that person: what you do, whether you are open right now, and a phone number that dials with one tap. Everything else, the story of the business, the photo gallery, the specials, can wait below.

The hours line is the most important sentence on the page

Owners are careful with logos and photos and careless with hours. Then hours change for a holiday or a staffing gap, the website still shows the old times, and a customer drives over to a locked door. Here is what a late-hours business site should do:

  • Show hours as real text, not inside an image, so phones and search engines can read them.
  • Spell out late nights: "open until 2 a.m. Friday and Saturday" beats a small grid nobody reads.
  • Say what happens after hours: voicemail, a callback by morning, an emergency number or an AI agent that answers.
  • Match your Google Business Profile exactly, including holiday changes.
  • Put a short banner on the home page when something changes, and take it down when it is over.

Google says local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and its advice for doing well starts with complete, accurate business information. Hours are part of that. We will not promise a ranking, because nobody honest can. We will make sure your site and your profile tell the same story.

Visitors in town for Beale Street, the blues and the barbecue

On December 15, 1977, an act of Congress declared Beale Street the official Home of the Blues, and the street still pulls visitors downtown for live music, then on to a plate of ribs somewhere across town. Those guests arrive with a phone, a hotel address and a few hours to spend.

The numbers back it up. In Tennessee's 2024 county tourism figures, as reported by local news, Shelby County, home of Memphis, ranked second in the state, behind only Davidson County (Nashville) and ahead of Sevier County and the Smokies resort towns. Statewide, Tennessee counted 147 million visitors and $31.66 billion in direct visitor spending that year.

A visitor does not know your street, your parking or whether you take reservations. A restaurant, tour, shop or music venue that serves them needs a page that answers those questions in the first screen, a menu or price list as text rather than a blurry photo, and a map. Write it so someone who has never been to Memphis can find the door.

Restaurants and barbecue joints

Menu as text, hours by day, whether to call ahead, and what to expect on a busy weekend.

Tours and music venues

Times, what is included, how to book or buy, and where to park or get dropped off.

Shops downtown

What you sell, the cross streets, and hours that hold true on game and festival nights.

Every part of the metro has its own name

Memphis customers think in neighborhoods and suburbs, and they often search with those names. A page for each area you really serve beats a single line that says "Greater Memphis."

  • In the cityDowntown, Midtown, Cooper-Young, Overton Square, Orange Mound and Whitehaven. If your shop is in one of them, name it in the page title and the first paragraph, with cross streets and parking.
  • SuburbsGermantown, Bartlett and Collierville. A trade or service that drives out to customers here deserves a page for each, written about that town.
  • Across the state lineSouthaven, in Mississippi. If you work there, say so plainly, since a customer there may assume a Memphis business will not come.
