A city of old houses keeps a lot of trades busy

Cleveland had about 365,000 residents in the Census Bureau's 2024 estimate, living in a housing stock that predates most of the country's. In the American Community Survey for 2012 to 2016, 53.6% of the city's 211,387 housing units were built in 1939 or earlier. Across the wider Cleveland area, the median occupied home was built in 1959 in the 2011 American Housing Survey, against 1974 nationally.

Old houses mean steady work: boilers and radiators, galvanized pipe, slate and multi-layer roofs, sagging porches, knob-and-tube wiring, wood windows, tuckpointing on brick foundations. A homeowner with one of those problems searches for the specific job, not just "contractor." The site that names the job, with photos of that kind of work, gets the call.

Lead-safe rules created a line of work that customers search for

Cleveland requires owners of rental units built before 1978 to prove those units are lead safe. Owners submit a clearance exam or a lead risk assessment done by a private, state-approved professional, renew every two years through the Department of Building and Housing, and owner-occupied homes are exempt. Enforcement began March 31, 2021, phased in by ZIP code, with every rental unit due by March 31, 2023.

That rule sent a lot of landlords looking for help: assessors, painters and contractors who do lead hazard control, and the people who handle window and trim replacement. If you offer any of that, your site should say so in the landlord's own words, name the paperwork you can help with, and list the neighborhoods you cover. A page like that answers a search a general "home improvement" page never will.

  • A separate page for each service, from boiler repair to lead hazard control.
  • Photos of real jobs, and a short note on what was done.
  • A form that asks for the address, the year the house was built and the problem.
  • The neighborhoods and suburbs you drive to, named one by one.

Clinics, practices and the businesses around the hospitals

Health care is the other big story in Cleveland's economy. In the state's December 2022 Ohio Major Employers Report, Cleveland Clinic topped the list with 56,986 Ohio employees, ahead of Walmart's 55,262. Around the big systems are many small, independent practices: dentists, physical therapists, chiropractors, counselors, home care agencies and medical billing offices.

Those practices compete for attention with hospital systems that have entire marketing departments. They will not out-spend them. What they can do is be clearer: who the provider is, which insurance they take, what a first visit looks like, where to park, and a phone number that is one tap away. Patients choosing between a big system and a small practice often decide on exactly those details.
