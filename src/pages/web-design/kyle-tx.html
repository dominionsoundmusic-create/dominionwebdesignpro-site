Growing faster than the water supply

The Census Bureau counted 28,016 people in Kyle in 2010 and 45,697 in 2020, and estimated 62,548 by mid-2023. One detail shows what that pace feels like from the inside. In December 2022, Kyle used up its annual allotment of Edwards Aquifer water and had to buy more from San Marcos. In 2024 both city councils approved a deal letting Kyle draw up to 500 acre-feet a year from its neighbor's permit through 2026, while new supply came online through the Alliance Regional Water Authority.

The same thing happens in commerce. New subdivisions open, and for months the families in them drive somewhere else for groceries, haircuts and repairs because nobody local has made it easy to find them. The businesses that get established early in a new neighborhood tend to keep those customers for years.

Do the free part first: your Google listing

Before you pay anyone for a website, Kyle or otherwise, spend an evening on your Google Business Profile. In a town where new residents search on their phones, the map results with business listings often show up above the regular web results, and the profile is free.

  • Choose the primary category that matches what most customers want from you, not the broadest one.
  • Set your real hours, including holidays, and your service area if you go to customers.
  • Add photos of your actual storefront, vehicles, team and work. Fresh photos help people recognize you.
  • Write a short description in plain words: what you do, where, and for whom.
  • Keep your phone number and website link current, because Google no longer offers chat messaging on business profiles.
  • Ask satisfied customers for a review, and reply to every review, good or bad, politely.

Then the website's job is to back that listing up: the same name, phone and hours, and pages that give more detail than a listing can. On the Pro package, Google Business Profile setup is part of the build.
