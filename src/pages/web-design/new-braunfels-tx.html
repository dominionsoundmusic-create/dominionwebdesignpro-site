Two economies in one town

New Braunfels sits where the Comal River meets the Guadalupe, and for generations it has run on water recreation, German heritage, festivals and the visitors they bring. That trade is still huge. What has changed is the other economy: the people who live here year round.

The U.S. Census Bureau counted 90,403 residents in 2020 and estimated 110,958 by mid-2023. The city's own financial report notes that its growth since 2010, about 92 percent, has been more than three times the rate of the wider San Antonio and New Braunfels metro area.

So a New Braunfels business usually serves one of two very different searchers. The visitor wants to know what it costs and whether it is open today. The new resident wants someone reliable to come back to for years. The website has to be built for the right one.

What a tourism-facing site has to answer

Visitors ask the same handful of questions and abandon the sites that will not answer them. Most local sites open with a paragraph about the owner's passion instead. The passion matters. It belongs further down.

What does it cost?

A price, a range or a "from" figure. Visitors comparing tube rentals or tours will not call to ask.

Are you open today?

Today's hours, not general ones, and a clear note when the season changes.

Do I need to book?

A booking link or a plain "walk-ins welcome," so nobody drives over and gets turned away.

Where do I park?

On a summer Saturday near the rivers, this question decides more visits than the menu does.

Is it right for kids?

Age limits, life jacket rules, high chairs, strollers. Families check before they come.

What if it rains?

Refund and reschedule policy in one sentence, easy to find.

Seasonal hours deserve their own warning

Nothing costs a tourism business more than a website still showing last summer's schedule. A family drives in from Houston, finds the doors locked, and leaves a one-star review that sits on your listing for years.

If your hours change through the year, keep them in one obvious place on the site and on your Google Business Profile, and update both on the same day. On a Dominion site, updates are part of hosting and maintenance, so a text or an email to Maurice is enough. The hard part is remembering, so put it on the calendar when each season starts.

For the residents' side: a plumber's site, for example

The tourism trade gets the attention, but a big share of New Braunfels businesses never see a tuber. A plumbing company serving the new subdivisions has a completely different job for its website.

  1. Lead with emergenciesA burst pipe on a Sunday is when most people meet their plumber. Put the phone number and "we answer any hour" up top if it is true.
  2. One page per job typeWater heaters, drain cleaning, slab leaks, new construction. Each answers the questions homeowners ask about that job.
  3. Say where you goNew Braunfels, Canyon Lake, Seguin, Schertz, Garden Ridge: whichever you really serve, named plainly.
  4. Show the licenseTexas plumbers are licensed by the state. Put your license number on the site where people can see it.
  5. Show real workPhotos from local jobs, and your Google reviews once you have them, displayed on the site with the Pro package.

None of that is glamorous, and that is the point. A homeowner with water on the kitchen floor is not browsing. They want to know you serve their street, that you are licensed, and how fast you can get there.

Business support in New Braunfels

The Greater New Braunfels Chamber of Commerce is one of the partners behind the New Braunfels Economic Development Corporation, along with the City of New Braunfels, Comal County, the Greater New Braunfels Economic Development Foundation and New Braunfels Utilities. The chamber also names a Small Business Person of the Year, open to owners with fewer than 50 employees who have run their business for at least five years.
