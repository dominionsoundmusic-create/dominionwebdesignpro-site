The Wedding Capital of Texas, by law

In 2015 the Texas Legislature passed House Concurrent Resolution 43, designating Dripping Springs the Wedding Capital of Texas. The governor signed it on May 18, 2015. The resolution notes that the area hosts more than a thousand weddings a year at more than 30 event venues.

Each of those weddings involves a long chain of local businesses: the venue, the planner, florist, photographer, caterer, baker, DJ or band, hair and makeup, rentals, transportation, lodging for guests and the restaurant for the rehearsal dinner. Almost every one of them is chosen by someone who lives somewhere else, comparing options on a screen.

If your site is slow, short on photos or vague about price and capacity, you are eliminated before anyone calls, and you never find out it happened.

What a venue or wedding vendor site has to do

  • Show every space, honestly. Ceremony site, reception hall, getting-ready rooms, the view at sunset, the rain plan. For venues and photographers the gallery is the product.
  • State capacity and a starting price. A couple with 140 guests and a fixed budget is filtering hard. A clear starting figure loses the inquiries you were going to lose anyway and wins the ones who fit.
  • Load fast on a phone. Much of this browsing happens in the evening on a couch. A gallery that takes six seconds to appear is a gallery nobody sees.
  • Make availability easy to ask about. A date field in the inquiry form, and a promise of when you will reply.
  • Have a form that actually works. Test it monthly. A silently broken form costs bookings you will never hear about.
  • Give directions from the cities couples live in. Drive times from Austin and the airport, and what the last mile of road is like.

Web design in Dripping Springs, TX beyond weddings

Weddings get the attention, but Dripping Springs is a destination in its own right. Tasting rooms, distilleries, breweries, restaurants, boutiques and short-term rentals all serve visitors who decided where to go before they got in the car. Those visitors ask a narrow set of questions: are you open today, do I need a reservation, can I bring kids or a dog, and where do I park.

Then there is the historic center. The Mercer Street Historic District is on the National Register of Historic Places, covering several blocks of Mercer Street and parts of nearby Wallace, San Marcos and College streets. US 290 used to run along Mercer Street itself before the highway was moved, and today Mercer runs just beside it. That means travelers on 290 are close to downtown shops and restaurants but cannot see them from the road. A visitor who searches "lunch in Dripping Springs" from the highway is exactly the customer a Mercer Street site should win.

And as more families move out along US 290, RR 12 and the roads west of Austin, demand grows for everyday services too: plumbers, pool companies, landscapers, pediatric dentists and pet care. Those businesses need a different kind of site from a venue, built around service areas and fast quotes rather than galleries.

What we would tell a venue not to buy

Wedding businesses get pitched constantly, and some of what they are sold does not help. A few honest warnings, even though they cost us nothing to skip.

  • Video backgrounds on the home pageThey look lovely on a fast office connection and stall on a phone with two bars of signal out on a ranch road. Use a strong still photo and put the video further down.
  • Piles of thin location pagesA page for every town within 200 miles, all saying the same thing, rarely helps. A handful of real pages, for the cities your couples actually come from, does better.
  • Hiding price behind a formSome planners insist on it. A starting figure filters out bad fits and saves you hours of replies.
  • A site you do not ownIf a marketplace or platform holds your pages, your reviews and your domain, leaving means starting over. Your own domain, in your own name, is worth protecting.

What does help: one excellent gallery per space, a short page for each type of event you host, a page on lodging and travel for out-of-town guests, and an inquiry form that asks the date, guest count and budget up front. That is the shape most venue sites should take, and it fits comfortably in the Pro package.
