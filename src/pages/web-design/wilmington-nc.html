Web design in Wilmington, NC for a town with two seasons

Tourism is big here, and it is measured. Visitors spent about $1.2 billion in New Hanover County in 2025, up 5.8% from the year before, which put the county seventh among North Carolina's 100 counties. In 2024 food and beverage was the largest spending category, with restaurants alone above $350 million, and tourism supported about 7,000 jobs in the county.

Those dollars arrive in waves. Carolina Beach and Kure Beach fill up on summer weekends, charter boats book out, short-term rentals turn over every few days. After Labor Day, much of the visitor trade thins and the local customer carries the business until spring.

Think of a rental turnover cleaner. In July the site needs a fast quote form for property managers and a clear list of the beach towns covered. In February it should talk to year-round homeowners about deep cleans and move-outs. Same business, same phone number, a different front page.

A website that never changes its message is stuck in one season. Planning for both from the start costs nothing extra; it just takes a designer who asks how your year actually goes.

How one coastal site can switch with the season

  1. Spring, before the crowdsOpen bookings for summer, post your season hours and refresh photos while people are still planning their trips.
  2. Summer, at full speedKeep the top of the home page short: hours, a map, a tap-to-call button and what is available this week. Visitors decide in seconds.
  3. Fall, the turnSwap visitor offers for local ones: lessons, maintenance plans, gift cards, a loyalty line for neighbors.
  4. Winter, the quiet monthsWrite the pages you never have time for in July, such as a service page for each area you cover. If a storm closes you for a day, the same notice spot tells customers when you reopen.

Wilmington's screen industry started with one movie. Frank Capra Jr. scouted the area in 1983 for Firestarter, made for Dino De Laurentiis, and De Laurentiis then built a studio near the airport. The studio changed hands several times, became EUE/Screen Gems after 1996, and in 2023 was sold to Cinespace, which runs it as Cinespace Wilmington.

The work comes and goes. The Wilmington Regional Film Commission's director estimated about $30 million in local production spending in 2025, against roughly $200 million the year before, and the state budget signed in 2026 raised the maximum production grant from $7 million to $20 million to win more shoots back.

The city had about 122,700 residents in the Census Bureau's 2023 estimate, but the customer base runs well past the city line, and people search by the name of their own spot on the map.
