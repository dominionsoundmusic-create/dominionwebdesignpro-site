A field town, on paper and on the road

HUD's January 2024 housing market profile describes the Odessa metro, which covers all of Ector County, as an economy that expands and contracts with oil prices, and its list of largest employers is led by oilfield service and industrial construction companies. Around them sit the welding shops, hot shot haulers, machine shops, tire and truck repair bays, and the taquerias and breakfast spots that open early for crews.

You can read it in the real estate along the main roads: industrial yards and shop buildings on Andrews Highway, retail and warehouse space along East 42nd Street, offices and clinics on North Grandview. The city counted 115,743 people in the Census Bureau's 2023 estimate, and 61.2% of residents are Hispanic or Latino, so plenty of customers and job seekers grew up switching between English and Spanish.

Two jobs, one website

Winning the work

  • A services page that names each job you do, written the way a buyer would search for it.
  • A coverage map or county list: Ector, Midland, Andrews, Winkler, Ward, wherever your trucks really go.
  • Your safety program and any training your crews hold, stated plainly and accurately.
  • A bid or quote request form that reaches your inbox, plus one-tap calling for the urgent ones.

Filling the trucks

  • A careers page with each open role, the license or experience it takes, and the schedule.
  • A short application form, so a driver can apply from a phone in a parking lot.
  • Photos of your actual yard, shop and equipment, which tell an applicant more than a paragraph.
  • A standing note for when you are not hiring, so good people can still reach you later.

Most website templates have no place for that second column. We build it in from the start, and changing the openings is part of routine updates.

Who is training the next hire

Odessa's schools feed the same workforce your careers page is trying to reach. Odessa College runs a Truck Driving Academy whose course of up to 200 hours prepares students to test for a Class A commercial driver's license, and Transport Topics reported that the Permian Strategic Partnership gave the academy $3.5 million to add trucks, instructors and scholarships as oilfield demand for drivers grew. The University of Texas Permian Basin is here in Odessa as well. Listing the licenses and certificates you want, in the same words those programs use, helps their graduates find your openings.

Workforce Solutions Permian Basin, the local arm of the Texas Workforce Commission, has an Odessa office on JBS Parkway, and employers can post jobs on WorkInTexas.com and get recruiting help at no cost. Your own careers page is not a replacement for that, but it is where an applicant goes to decide whether you are worth calling.
