Greensboro's making history, in four dates

YearWhat happenedWhy it still matters to an owner
1905The Cone family opened the White Oak denim plant, alongside its Proximity and Revolution mills.Greensboro built a workforce and a supply chain around textiles that outlasted the mills themselves.
1960On February 1, four N.C. A&T students sat at the whites-only Woolworth lunch counter on South Elm Street and refused to leave. The store desegregated that July.The building is now the International Civil Rights Center and Museum, which opened on the 50th anniversary in 2010 and brings visitors downtown.
2017White Oak closed at the end of the year, with about 200 jobs lost. It was described as the last selvedge denim mill operating in the United States.The big mills are gone, so the shops and trades that remain compete for new kinds of buyers.
2025Toyota began building batteries in November at its $13.9 billion plant in Liberty, with up to 5,100 jobs expected.Big plants buy from smaller local suppliers and service firms, who need to be easy to find and vet.

Web design in Greensboro, NC for shops that sell to other businesses

A homeowner hiring a plumber judges by photos and reviews. A purchasing manager judging a supplier wants facts in a hurry: what you can make, in what materials, at what volume, how fast, and who to contact. If those answers are buried in a paragraph about passion and quality, the buyer moves on to the next result.

A good capability page reads like a spec sheet written for people. It lists processes and equipment, materials, the sizes or tolerances you can actually hold, typical quantities and lead times, the counties you serve, and any certifications or safety programs you genuinely have. A short request-for-quote form at the bottom does the rest.

Pair that page with a few photos of real work, an about page that names the owner, and a contact page with a phone that taps to call. That is a complete industrial site for a small Greensboro shop, and it fits in the five pages of Starter.

The Triad's buyers come twice a year and every weekday

Greensboro shares a metro area with High Point, home of the High Point Market. The furniture trade show runs each April and October and draws tens of thousands of buyers, designers and exhibitors to the area. Upholsterers, freight and installation crews, photographers, caterers and short-term rental hosts all feel those weeks.

Then there is everyday demand from a city of real size. The Census Bureau estimated Greensboro at about 302,000 residents in 2023. N.C. A&T reported 15,275 students in fall 2025, the first time any historically Black college or university has enrolled more than 15,000, and by its own count it has been the nation's largest public HBCU for more than a decade.

Local help for Greensboro owners

SBTDC at N.C. A&T

The state's Small Business and Technology Development Center office hosted at A&T serves Guilford, Randolph, Rockingham and Caswell counties. Its counselors help with financing, financial performance and market opportunities.

City supplier programs

The city has long run a minority and women owned business program for city contracts. In May 2026 it also launched a race and gender neutral Small Business Enterprise program, with a city vendor directory open to firms in Guilford, Rockingham and Randolph counties.

Greensboro Chamber of Commerce

Runs networking, advocacy and the Launch Greensboro track for entrepreneurs, including LaunchCamp and LaunchLab, plus a yearly Small Business Awards program.
