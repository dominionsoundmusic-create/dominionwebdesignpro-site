The week Gilmer is not a small town

Gilmer had 4,843 residents at the 2020 census. Then the East Texas Yamboree comes around in October and the courthouse square and city park fill with a crowd the town could never produce on its own. The festival has run since October 1935, when Upshur County farmers, newly free of a sweet potato weevil quarantine, started a fall celebration of the harvest. It still comes back every October, with a queen's coronation and several days of events around the square.

Look at it as a business owner rather than a local. For a few days, thousands of people who do not live here are walking around with a phone, looking for somewhere to eat, somewhere to stay, something to buy, and somebody who can fix whatever just broke on the drive in. They do not know your business exists. If you do not show up when they search, that money goes to whoever does.

A Yamboree checklist for your website

  • Live and indexed by late summer, not the week of the festival. New pages take time to show up in search.
  • Festival hours posted plainly, plus any days you are closed.
  • Where you are in relation to the square, in words a visitor understands.
  • A phone number that dials with one tap, at the top of every page.
  • Your Google Business Profile matching the site exactly: name, hours, phone.

Customers here drive to you, and they look you up before they get in the truck. A rancher in Harmony or a family in Union Grove is not going to make the trip on a guess.

Gilmer also sits on U.S. 271 within reach of Longview and Tyler, which cuts both ways. Your customers will drive to the bigger towns, and businesses from those towns show up in Gilmer searches without trying. The only real defense is a site that says clearly, on its own pages, that you are here and what you do.

What to gather before you ask for the demo

You do not need anything to get the demo itself; your business name and what you do are enough. When you decide to go ahead, the build moves fastest if you have a few things together. A list of every service you offer, in your own words, even if it is scribbled on a legal pad. The towns you will actually drive to. Your hours, including how they change in October or during deer season. Whatever photos you have of your work, your shop or your crew; phone photos are fine. And the login for your domain if you already own one, or the name you want if you do not.
