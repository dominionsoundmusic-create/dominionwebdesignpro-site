Good web design Fort Worth Texas owners can be proud of starts with a simple fact: Fort Worth is not a suburb of anywhere. It has its own downtown, its own history and its own customers, and the people searching for a roofer, a cafe or a bookkeeper there tend to type "Fort Worth", or the name of their part of town, rather than "DFW". A site that files you under the whole Metroplex makes those searchers work harder to figure out whether you are close.

A city of a million that still gets called "the other half"

Fort Worth crossed a line most Texans did not expect it to cross so soon. The city reports a population of 1,008,106 as of July 1, 2024, the first time it reached a million, and it gained 23,442 residents in that single year. That is bigger than many state capitals, and yet outsiders still talk about it as the western end of Dallas.

Locals do not. Fort Worth has a western identity it has kept on purpose, along with neighborhoods and districts that each pull their own crowd. For a small business, that pride is useful. A customer who thinks of themselves as a Fort Worth person, or a Near Southside person, notices when a website talks to them in those terms, and notices just as quickly when it does not.

Fort Worth website design that does not hide you in the Metroplex

Google explains its local results with three ideas: relevance, which is how well a listing matches the search; distance from the searcher or the place named; and prominence, which is how well known a business is. When someone types "electrician Camp Bowie" or "fort worth website design", the place name is doing a lot of the work. A site that mentions Fort Worth once, in the footer, gives Google little to match.

So we put the city and the parts of it you serve where they belong: the page title, the first paragraph, the service area page and the contact page, all in plain sentences. Nobody can promise a ranking, and we will not. What we can do is make the site clearly about Fort Worth work.

Where Fort Worth owners can get free or low-cost help

A website is one tool. These local programs help with the rest, from business plans to city contracts:

  • Devoyd Jennings Business Assistance CenterRun by the City of Fort Worth on the James E. Guinn Entrepreneurial Campus, with training, technical assistance and leads on funding for startups and established firms.
  • Small Business Development ProgramThe city's program for small firms, with support services delivered through local chambers, including the Fort Worth Hispanic Chamber and the Fort Worth Metropolitan Black Chamber. Check the city's page for what is offered this year.
  • Tarrant Small Business Development CenterPart of the North Texas SBDC network, offering free, confidential one-on-one advising and training for owners who live or do business in Tarrant County. It also has a desk at the Business Assistance Center.

Texas has more than 3 million small businesses, firms with fewer than 500 employees, according to the SBA Office of Advocacy's state profile. Plenty of them are competing for the same Fort Worth searches you are.
