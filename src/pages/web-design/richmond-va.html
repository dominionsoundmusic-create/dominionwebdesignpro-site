From biscuit factory to beverage district

Scott's Addition is the clearest picture of the Richmond maker economy. Planned as a residential neighborhood, it was zoned for industry in 1927, and by the 1930s it held a Nabisco factory, a Coca-Cola bottling plant and a Chevrolet parts depot. Those businesses later left, the buildings aged, and the turnaround came through historic district status and tax credits for rehabilitating old structures.

The first craft brewery there opened in October 2013. A cidery moved into a 1940 granite complex that once held the city's stables in 2015. Today locals call the area the Scott's Addition Beverage District, with breweries, cideries, a meadery and a distillery sharing blocks with remaining industrial tenants. Every one of those producers depends on visitors who looked them up first.

Web design in Richmond for small producers

A brewery, cidery, roaster, candle maker or hot sauce company does not need what a national brand needs. It needs a handful of pages that answer the same questions customers ask at the bar every week.

  1. Tasting room or shop hours, front and centerIncluding the days you close, the kitchen or food truck hours if they differ, and whether kids and dogs are welcome.
  2. A where-to-buy pageThe stores, restaurants and markets that carry your product, grouped by area, so a fan can find you in Henrico or Chesterfield as easily as in the city.
  3. An events calendar that stays currentRelease days, live music, trivia and private bookings, with past events taken down so nobody shows up for last month's party.
  4. A wholesale and private events inquiry formA short form that sends a bar manager or a wedding planner straight to your inbox with the details you need.

What it usually does not need is a giant online store. If you sell a few items for pickup or ship merchandise, that can be added later. Start with the pages that answer the questions.

Carytown and the independent shop

Carytown is one stretch of West Cary Street, about nine blocks from the Powhite Parkway to the Boulevard, nicknamed the "Mile of Style." It holds more than 230 shops, restaurants and offices, most of them independently owned, plus the Byrd Theatre, which has shown movies since 1928. A local guide notes many shops close or shorten hours on Mondays and that street parking fills up on weekends.

Those two details are exactly what a shop website should answer. A customer driving in from Midlothian does not want to find a locked door. The site should give the hours by day, mention where people usually park, describe what you carry in plain words, and show a few honest photos. That is a five-page Starter site, not a big project.

Jackson Ward and a long history of Black-owned business

Jackson Ward was the center of Black business life in Richmond. Maggie L. Walker chartered the St. Luke Penny Savings Bank there in 1903 and became its first president, the first African American woman to charter a bank in the United States. The bank later merged with two other Black-owned banks to form Consolidated Bank and Trust. Her home is now a National Historic Site.

Richmond website design for customers who never say "Richmond"

Richmond is an independent city that belongs to no county, with Henrico County and Chesterfield County wrapped around it. Short Pump, the busy retail area about 13 miles west, is in Henrico. Midlothian is an unincorporated community in Chesterfield. Inside the city, Manchester on the south bank of the James was its own city until it merged with Richmond in 1910, and Church Hill, the Fan and Jackson Ward each have their own pull.

Google ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence. A customer in Midlothian searching for a gym or a photographer gets results near Midlothian.
