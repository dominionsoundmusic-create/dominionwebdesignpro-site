Greenville web design for a downtown people walk

Downtown Greenville was redesigned for walking on purpose. A streetscape plan put in place starting in 1979 cut Main Street from four traffic lanes to two, added angled parking and lined the street with trees, and the city later opened the river up as well: Falls Park on the Reedy and its curved Liberty Bridge, a footbridge that replaced a highway bridge over the falls, opened in September 2004. Today restaurants, shops and offices sit within a few blocks of each other along Main Street and down toward the falls.

A walking customer behaves differently from a driving one. She is close, she is in a hurry and she is comparing you with the place across the street.

For that customer the top of your home page matters more than anything below it: what you are, whether you are open right now, how far away you are and a button to call. Everything else, the story, the gallery, the full menu, can sit lower.

Speed matters too. A walking customer on a phone signal will leave a slow page before it loads. We build lean, hand-written pages and compress every photo, so the first screen appears quickly.

A trail-side shop, walked through

The Prisma Health Swamp Rabbit Trail is a rail trail whose continuous stretch between Greenville and Travelers Rest runs about 18 miles. In Travelers Rest, the state tourism office has credited the trail with drawing restaurants, coffee shops, a brewery, a distillery, galleries and other shops to Main Street. TrailLink also notes that parts of the route are subject to flooding, which is one more reason a trail-side business needs a place on its site to post today's status.

  1. The searchA rider near Travelers Rest types "bike repair near me" or the shop's own name while stopped on the Swamp Rabbit Trail.
  2. The first screenThe site shows today's hours, a one-line list of repairs done same day, and a call button. No pop-ups, no slow video.
  3. The decisionA short page says which repairs need an appointment and which do not, so the rider knows whether to stop now or come back.
  4. The follow-upA contact form emails the owner every question that comes in after hours, so the rider who could not stop today can book for the weekend.

Swap the bike shop for a cafe, a brewery or an outfitter and the outline barely changes. What changes is the words, and we write those for you.

South Carolina runs on small firms. The SBA Office of Advocacy's 2025 profile counts 530,402 small businesses in the state, 99.4% of all businesses, employing 42.9% of the state's workers. Most of them need the same thing from a website: to be understood quickly by someone nearby.

Whether you are in Greenville, Spartanburg, Anderson or a smaller Upstate town, the work is the same. If you are still working on a plan or financing, local help exists.
