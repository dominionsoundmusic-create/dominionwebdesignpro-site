Cedar Park now sits third in the Austin metro

The Greater Austin Chamber lists Cedar Park as the third largest city in the Austin metropolitan area, with a population just under 84,000. The 2020 census counted 77,595. It sits on Austin's northern edge in Williamson County, about 17 miles from downtown, and much of its commerce runs along a few roads that everyone in town knows by heart.

Bell Boulevard, the old US 183 route through town, holds decades of locally owned shops, restaurants and service businesses. Whitestone Boulevard (RM 1431) carries the bigger retail centers and medical offices. The 183A toll road brought newer shopping, the 1890 Ranch center and office parks to the east side, and Lakeline straddles the border with Austin.

Which corridor you sit on changes who searches for you. A Bell Boulevard barber gets the commuter who wants a cut on the way home. A clinic on Whitestone gets families from the subdivisions north of 1431. Your pages should be written for that person, not for "Central Texas" in general.

What is changing on Bell Boulevard and around town

Cedar Park has spent years planning bigger changes to its commercial core. Owners who are online and easy to find before those changes land are in a better spot than those who wait.

The Bell Boulevard redevelopment

City Council approved a plan to realign Bell Boulevard between Park Street and Cypress Creek Road, freeing roughly 40 acres for mixed-use development and setting aside about 12 acres as the Buttercup Creek Natural Area. Road projects like that reroute traffic for a long stretch. When drivers cannot see you from the road, search is how they find you.

New employers and visitors

The city's Economic Development Corporation, funded by a half-cent sales tax, helped bring Firefly Aerospace's headquarters to Cedar Park. Perfect Game's planned baseball and softball complex off Ronald Reagan Boulevard has a groundbreaking reported for 2027. Visitors to tournaments will search for food, lodging and services from their phones.

What one Cedar Park service page should look like

Here is the outline Maurice would start from for, say, an air conditioning company based off Whitestone Boulevard. Every business is different, but the shape shows what "hand-written" means in practice.

  1. A heading that says the service and the place"AC repair in Cedar Park and Leander," not "Comfort solutions you can trust."
  2. The first two sentences answer the searchWhat you fix, how soon you can come out, and how to call. Someone with a dead unit in August reads nothing else.
  3. How pricing worksA diagnostic fee, a range for common repairs, or a plain note that quotes are free. Hiding it sends people to the competitor who did not.
  4. Where you goCedar Park neighborhoods and nearby towns you really cover, in a short sentence, not a wall of 40 zip codes.
  5. Proof you can showReal photos of your trucks and crew, your license number where the state requires one, and your Google reviews once you have them.
  6. Common questionsThree or four questions your office hears every week, answered the way you would answer them on the phone.

A template site gives you the heading and maybe a stock photo. The rest is what customers actually decide on, and it is what we write.
