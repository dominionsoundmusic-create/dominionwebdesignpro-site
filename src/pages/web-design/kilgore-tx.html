Oil built Kilgore, and it still shapes who buys from you

On October 3, 1930, "Dad" Joiner's Daisy Bradford No. 3 came in on a Rusk County farm and set off the East Texas oil boom. Kilgore sat in the thick of it. Wells crowded so tightly into downtown that one plot of about 1.2 acres became known as the World's Richest Acre, and today it is a park among the steel derricks that still stand over the middle of town.

The boom faded, but the industry did not leave. Oil and gas service companies, machine shops, trucking outfits and supply houses still work out of Kilgore. They sell to other businesses, and for them a website does a different job: it is the background check a buyer runs before picking up the phone. A plain page listing your services, your equipment and your service area does more there than any slogan.

A college sits in the middle of town

Kilgore College is the other engine. In 1939 the college hired Gussie Nell Davis, and in September 1940 her Rangerettes gave their first performance, the first women's drill team to take the field at a football game. The Rangerettes are still the college's calling card, and the summer Texas Shakespeare Festival, which the college started in 1986 for the Texas Sesquicentennial, brings a theater crowd to campus every June and July.

For a business, a college means a population that turns over every year. Each fall a new group of students and their parents arrive knowing nothing about where to eat, where to get a phone screen fixed or which apartment manager returns calls. They have never heard your name from a neighbor. They search, read two or three sites, and choose. The businesses near campus that have a clear site and an accurate map listing get that choice; the ones that rely on everybody already knowing them do not.

The same goes for the families who drive in for a Rangerette performance or a weekend of plays. They plan dinner and a place to stay from a hotel room or a parking lot, often an hour before they need it, so a site that loads quickly and shows hours and a phone number at the top is worth more to them than any amount of design polish.
