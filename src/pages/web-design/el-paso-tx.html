What drives business in El Paso

The Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas names Fort Bliss as El Paso's largest employer and an important generator of local economic activity. Transportation and logistics matter too, because of the border and the city's trading relationship with Ciudad Juárez, which makes El Paso one of the major crossing points on the U.S.-Mexico border. Manufacturing across the river feeds El Paso's service businesses as well.

That mix produces a particular set of small businesses. Military families moving in and out need movers, mechanics, home services, child care and dentists, and they search for them fast because they are new in town. Logistics and trade create work for trucking companies, repair shops, and the restaurants and offices around them. And a large, settled bilingual population supports the salons, clinics, contractors and family restaurants that every neighborhood has.

The geography adds its own pattern. Kern Place, just east of the University of Texas at El Paso and west of downtown, mixes older homes with a small entertainment district at its edges, and North Mesa Street carries offices and retail through that side of town. Montana Avenue's central stretch, once a row of grand homes, has been turning into a commercial corridor since the 1990s, and farther east it is being rebuilt as an expressway through fast-growing Far East El Paso. A business on the Westside and one on the Eastside can share a city and still serve almost entirely different customers.

Writing an El Paso website for a bilingual city

According to Census estimates for 2019 to 2023, 66.4 percent of El Paso residents five or older use a language besides English in their homes. That does not mean every page has to be translated, and it does not mean English is a barrier for most customers. It means the people reading your site move between two languages all day, and they notice whether a business seems to welcome both.

  • Say it on the page. If your staff speaks Spanish, say so near the top. "Se habla español" on a contractor's home page answers a question before it is asked.
  • Use the words people search. Some customers search in English, some in Spanish, many in a mix. Your service names should match how your customers actually talk.
  • Keep it simple on a phone. Short sentences, clear buttons and a visible phone number help every reader.
  • Mind the clock. Hours listed on your site should be El Paso time. Out-of-town customers calling from Central Time will appreciate the clarity.
