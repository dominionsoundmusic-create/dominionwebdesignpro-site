Downtown McKinney: a square that still does what it was planned to do

McKinney was founded in 1848 as the seat of Collin County, about 30 miles north of Dallas, and the courthouse square was the only public space set aside in the original town plan. Like many Texas downtowns it slumped when new highways drew traffic away, then came back. Today the district runs as a Main Street program, a designation conferred by the Texas Historical Commission and the National Trust for Historic Preservation, and its shops are mostly locally owned.

A few blocks away, Chestnut Square Historic Village hosts the farmers market and tours that include the 1854 Faires House, the oldest home in McKinney. Those draws bring people downtown who have never been before. They search on a phone for lunch, a gift shop or a place that is open past five, and they choose from what loads.

What a shop on the square needs online

  • Current hours, including special event hours, on the home page.
  • What you actually sell, with photos, so a visitor knows whether to walk over.
  • Where to park and how to find you from the courthouse.
  • A tap-to-call number and a map at the top, not buried in the footer.

An airport terminal on the way

McKinney National Airport has served private aircraft for years. In July 2025 the city broke ground on a passenger terminal of about 46,000 square feet with four gates, expandable to six, and a 980-space parking lot. Reports at the groundbreaking put the start of commercial service around late 2026, though the city still needed to sign an airline at that point.

If scheduled flights arrive, they bring visitors who land with no idea what is in town and a phone in their hand. That rewards the businesses that are easy to find: hotels and restaurants first, then car services, salons, florists and anyone a traveler might need on short notice. It also brings new businesses competing for the same searches. Either way, being findable in McKinney matters more each year, not less.
