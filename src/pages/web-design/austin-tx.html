Website design for Austin, TX businesses that sell to neighbors

Austin counted 961,855 residents in the 2020 census, which makes it a big city that still buys like a collection of neighborhoods. A taco trailer on East Cesar Chavez, a bike shop on South Lamar, a dentist off Burnet Road and an accountant near The Domain all draw most of their customers from a few miles around them. People search for "near me" and pick from what shows up.

That is the job a small business website has to do here. It needs to say what you do, which parts of town you cover, when you are open and how to reach you, in words a stranger can scan on a phone in ten seconds. A clever homepage animation does none of that. A clear services page with your hours and your neighborhood does.

When people search "web design in Austin" they find big agencies first, because those agencies pay to be found. Plenty of them do good work. Most of them are also priced for venture-funded startups and enterprise clients, not for a two-truck plumbing company in Pflugerville or a salon on North Loop.

Where Austin businesses trade, and what that means for your pages

Austin's commercial life is spread along corridors more than it is packed into a single downtown. A website that names the right ones reads like it was written by someone who looked at a map of your city, because it was.

  • South Congress and South LamarBoutiques, restaurants, salons and service shops that rely on walk-in visitors and on locals who search before they drive.
  • East AustinEast Sixth, East Cesar Chavez and Manor Road carry food, bars, studios and makers, many of them small and owner-run.
  • Burnet Road and North LamarLong, mixed corridors of auto shops, medical offices, restaurants and older strip centers with a steady neighborhood trade.
  • The Domain and North AustinOffices, retail and the professional services that serve the tech employers around them.
  • MuellerA planned neighborhood on the old airport site, with its own shops, clinics and family services.
  • South and Southeast AustinHome services, contractors and trades working out of shops and trucks, serving homeowners across the metro.

For a business in one of these areas, the service-area page and the contact page should name the neighborhoods you actually serve and nothing you do not. A roofer who works from Manor to Buda should say so. A bakery on Burnet Road should not pretend to serve Round Rock. Search engines and customers both reward that kind of plain accuracy.

Free small business help in Austin that most owners never use

None of these programs are connected to us, and we gain nothing from listing them. They are free or close to it, and they are worth an hour of your time before you spend money with anyone.

Austin Economic Development, Small Business Division

The city's small business team offers no-cost training and coaching on business planning, marketing, funding and technology, in person and online, through SmallBizAustin.org. Classes have been held at its training room at 5202 East Ben White Boulevard. It also runs Austin Small Business Week each May and a free Small Business Fundamentals Week early in the year.

SCORE Austin

Volunteer mentors, many of them retired owners and executives, meet with small business owners at no charge, however many sessions you need. Workshops are free or low cost. A good place to pressure-test a plan before you put money behind it.

Texas State University SBDC

The Small Business Development Center hosted by Texas State University offers confidential one-on-one business counseling at no or low cost, with locations that include Austin and Round Rock. It is funded in part by the U.S. Small Business Administration.

Why local search is harder in Austin than almost anywhere in Texas

Austin has a dense, well-funded web design and marketing industry, which means the agencies themselves are competing hard for every search. The same is true in most trades. A plumber, a lawyer or a med spa in Austin is up against dozens of competitors who have already paid someone to optimize their pages.

You do not beat that by out-spending them. You beat it by being specific. A page that says "drain cleaning in Hyde Park and Crestview, same week" is more useful to a searcher, and easier for Google to match, than a page that says "quality plumbing solutions for the greater Austin area." That specificity is what hand-written pages give you and template sites never do.

Three things decide most local results here, and only one of them is the website:

  1. Your Google Business ProfileCorrect name, category, hours, phone and service area, with real photos. It is free, and it drives the map results that sit above everything else.
  2. Your reviewsRecent, real reviews from real customers. Ask for them every time a job goes well.
  3. Your websiteFast on a phone, clear about what you do and where, with a page for each main service. That is the part we build.
