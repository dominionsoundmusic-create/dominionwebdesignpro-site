State Line Road runs through your customer list

State Line Road is exactly what it sounds like, and people in the metro cross it without thinking. KCUR, the local public radio station, has reported on residents who live on one side and work, shop and eat on the other every day. One Overland Park resident put it simply: Kansas City is one metropolitan area, not two separate places.

That is true for daily life and false for paperwork. Missouri and Kansas set different rules, and each city on each side sets its own. A website that ignores this either leaves out half the customers or confuses them. A customer in Brookside and a customer in Olathe want the same thing from your site: proof that you cover their side.

Same metro, different rules on each side

These are the differences that change what a small business website should say. Check the current rules with each office; this is a starting map, not legal advice.

What your site should sayThe KCMO neighborhoods and Missouri towns you serve, and your city license where it applies.The Kansas cities you serve by name, and the Johnson County or city license where it applies.

One helpful detail: KCMO built its residential contractor program to line up with Johnson County's and Independence's, so an exam passed in one does not have to be retaken in the others. Your website does not need to explain any of that. It just needs to be accurate about which licenses you hold and where.

Kansas City website design by neighborhood and suburb

Inside Kansas City, Missouri, people identify with their part of town. The Crossroads Arts District downtown is known for galleries, studios and its First Fridays. The River Market, along the Missouri River, grew up around its historic marketplace. Westport is one of the city's oldest districts, known for restaurants and nightlife. Brookside and Waldo, to the south, are walkable neighborhoods of local shops and restaurants. North of the river, the Northland is an informal name usually taken to mean Clay and Platte counties, part of it inside KCMO and part of it not.

Across State Line, the suburbs are cities with their own names and their own searches: Overland Park, Olathe, Lenexa, Leawood, Shawnee, Prairie Village. Johnson County keeps adding people; FOX4 reported it grew by about 6,074 residents from 2024 to 2025. A customer there types "electrician Olathe," not "electrician Kansas City."

Why the town name matters to Google

Google says three things mainly decide its local results: relevance, distance and prominence. Distance is measured from the searcher, or from the place named in the search. When someone in Lenexa searches for a service, a business whose site and Google Business Profile say Lenexa clearly is a better match than one that says only "Kansas City metro." No web designer can promise where you will rank. A clear page for each town you serve is something you can control.
