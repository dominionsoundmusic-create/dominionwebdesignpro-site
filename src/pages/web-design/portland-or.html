Six address sections, not four

Portland splits its streets into named sections, and people use them in conversation. "We are in Southeast" means something to a Portlander that a ZIP code does not. For decades the city had five: North, Northeast, Northwest, Southeast and Southwest. In 2020 the city created a sixth, South Portland, out of the part of Southwest that sits east of SW Naito Parkway, or east of SW View Point Terrace farther south. About 6,000 addresses in South Waterfront, Collins View and Riverdale changed. "SW" became "S," the leading zeros came off, the Postal Service accepted old addresses for one more year, and the last Southwest street signs there were scheduled to come down in May 2025.

If your business is in that area and your website, Google Business Profile or delivery instructions still say "SW" with a leading zero, fix it. A wrong section prefix can send a driver or a first-time customer to the other side of the river.

Portland Oregon website design for people who make things

Portland has a habit of making its own goods in small batches. Portland Made, a collective of local makers and manufacturers, was started in 2012 by Kelley Roy, founder of the ADX makerspace, to organize what was already a fast-growing movement. A 2018 Urban Manufacturing Alliance snapshot, done with Portland State University and Prosper Portland as local partners, found that the city's makers and manufacturers were growing but struggling to find affordable space inside the city.

That pressure shows up online. A maker who rents a small shared shop, sells at markets on weekends, ships to a few retailers and takes custom orders has four audiences with four different questions. The website has to answer all of them without becoming a maze.

For a shopper who met you at a market

Your name exactly as it was on the booth sign, photos of the real products, and the next place they can find you in person. Many of these visitors type your name from memory, so the site has to come up for close misspellings too.

For a shop buyer or a custom client

A plain wholesale or commission page: minimums, lead times, how to send a request, and a form that lands in your inbox. If you already sell through an outside store, the site links to it clearly. A cart is not something we build, and we say that up front.

Street fairs, studio nights and the hours around them

Some Portland neighborhoods run on events. On Alberta Street in Northeast, Last Thursday closes about fifteen blocks to traffic on summer evenings from 6 to 9 p.m., with galleries, food and artisans along the route. The Mississippi Street Fair in July draws more than 30,000 visitors to that close-in North Portland street.

Those nights bring in people who have never heard of you. The next morning some of them search for the shop they walked past. If your site shows your regular hours, your event hours and a photo of the storefront they will remember, you are easy to find again. If the site still lists last year's fair date, they move on.

Home service crews across the metro

Cleaners, painters, handymen and organizers work the whole metro, which the Census Bureau estimated at about 2.54 million people in 2024 across the Portland, Vancouver and Hillsboro area. A cleaning service in Gresham and one in Lake Oswego serve very different streets. Each site should name the towns and sections it really covers, and nothing it does not.

Web design Portland contractors need when the work crosses a river

The metro area spans two states. Vancouver, Washington is part of the same Census metro as Portland and Hillsboro, and many trades work on both sides of the Columbia, while others stay in Oregon and head west to Beaverton or east to Gresham. Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. A roofer whose site says only "serving the Portland area" gives Google little to match against a search typed in Vancouver or Hillsboro. A short, honest page for each place you truly serve does better than a long list of town names at the bottom of the home page.
