A border city whose main street is a bridge

The Laredo Economic Development Corporation describes the Port of Laredo as the busiest of all U.S. border crossings and third among more than 450 airports, seaports and border crossings nationwide. Port Laredo handled $353.94 billion in trade in 2025, and more than 97% of it was tied to Mexico. Trucks cross at the World Trade Bridge and the Colombia Solidarity Bridge, and Mines Road (FM 1472), which links the two, is lined with industrial parks and warehouses.

That traffic supports a long list of small firms: customs brokers, freight forwarders, carriers, cross-dock and warehouse operators, truck and trailer repair, and the restaurants, tire shops and services that drivers use. The Laredo Chamber of Commerce, founded in 1915, counts transportation and logistics companies among more than 700 members.

What a Laredo logistics website should show first

  1. What you handleCustoms entries, forwarding, transloading, dry van, flatbed, refrigerated, storage. Name the services in the words a shipping manager searches for, not just a slogan.
  2. Where and whenWhich crossings you work, your yard or warehouse location, your office hours and whether someone can be reached after hours when a load is stuck.
  3. Who will answerThe languages your staff speaks, a direct phone number and an email that goes to a person who can quote.
  4. How to get a rateA short quote form that asks for origin, destination, commodity and timing, and sends it straight to your inbox.
  5. Why a shipper can trust youYour real licenses, authority and insurance details, stated plainly. No stock claims.

Not every Laredo company works the trade directly.
