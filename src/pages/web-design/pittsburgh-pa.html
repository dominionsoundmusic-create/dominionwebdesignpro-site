Ninety neighborhoods, each with a name people search

The City of Pittsburgh officially recognizes 90 neighborhoods, inside a city of about 307,600 people in the Census Bureau's estimate for July 2025. The system goes back to 1969, when the city created a Neighborhood Planning Division under Mayor Pete Flaherty. Some of the 90 are halves or thirds of a place locals treat as one: Squirrel Hill is split into North and South, and Lawrenceville into Upper, Central and Lower.

That matters for a small business because customers search the way they talk. Someone in Greenfield wants an HVAC tech who works Greenfield. Someone on the North Side may never drive to the South Hills for a haircut. A site that says only "serving Pittsburgh" leaves both of them guessing whether you are close enough to bother calling.

Rivers, hills and tunnels change what "nearby" means

The Heinz History Center calls Pittsburgh the City of Bridges and ties the nickname to three major rivers and a landscape full of hills and ravines. A 2006 study counted 446 bridges inside city limits, a figure people still argue about. Tunnels do the rest. PennDOT runs the Fort Pitt, Squirrel Hill, Liberty and Stowe tunnels, and WESA reported in 2017 that more than 229,000 people drove through them each day. The Fort Pitt and Squirrel Hill tunnels pinch the Parkway traffic at rush hour, and the Liberty Tunnels connect the South Hills to the city.

For a service business, that turns "within 15 miles" into a meaningless promise. Fifteen miles one way can be an easy drive; the other way can be two bridges and a backed-up tunnel. Here is how we would translate that onto a page.

Web design Pittsburgh PA business streets ask for

Many of the city's neighborhoods have a main street with its own following. A site for a business on one of them should name the street early and plainly, because that is what regulars and visitors type.

  • Strip District and Lower LawrencevilleThe old warehouse and shipping ground along the Allegheny riverfront, now nicknamed Robotics Row for the robotics firms that moved into former factories there.
  • Butler Street, LawrencevilleWhere most of the neighborhood's shops, boutiques, galleries and restaurants sit.
  • Liberty Avenue, BloomfieldThe main strip of the neighborhood often called Pittsburgh's Little Italy, with Italian bakeries and restaurants mixed in among newer bars.
  • East Carson Street, South SideThe South Side's long main street and one of the city's busiest strips for restaurants, bars and nightlife.
  • Forbes and Murray Avenues, Squirrel HillThe two corridors where the neighborhood shops and eats.

Outside the city line the same rule holds. A customer in Mt. Lebanon or Cranberry searches for their own town, not for Pittsburgh, so a business that wants work there needs a page that says so and explains how it gets there.

Eds, meds and robots set the customer base

Pittsburgh's biggest employers are hospitals and universities. The Pittsburgh Business Times, as reported by WPXI, ranks UPMC as the region's largest employer and the largest nongovernmental employer in the state, with the University of Pittsburgh and Carnegie Mellon also high on the list. Carnegie Mellon's National Robotics Engineering Center has been in Lawrenceville since 1996, and the robotics companies along the riverfront grew up partly around it.

Two kinds of small business feel this most. The first sells to the institutions and the tech firms: cleaning crews, caterers, electricians, sign shops, couriers. Their buyers check a site for a clear list of services, the areas covered and a way to request a quote, so that is what the site should lead with. The second sells to the people who work there, often on hospital shifts that run nights and weekends. For them, hours written as text, a tap-to-call number and a form that works at 2 a.m. count for more than anything decorative.

How local search reads a city of neighborhoods

Google says local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. In Pittsburgh, distance is especially blunt: a searcher in Shadyside sees businesses near Shadyside first. Prominence builds over time through reviews and mentions. Relevance is the part your site controls, and it improves when your pages and your Google Business Profile say clearly what you do and in which neighborhoods.
