Long Beach website design for a city built around a port

Long Beach counted 466,742 residents in the 2020 census. Its economy has been tied to the harbor from the start: the Long Beach Area Chamber of Commerce began as a Board of Trade whose focus was largely the port, and it took its current name when it was incorporated in 1918. Today the Port of Long Beach is reported as the second busiest port in the nation, and it moved a record 9.6 million container units (TEUs) in 2024.

All that cargo supports a long chain of smaller firms: trucking companies, warehouses, customs brokers, equipment repair shops, staffing agencies and the lunch spots that feed their crews. Most of them win work from other businesses, and a logistics coordinator checking out a vendor wants specifics, not slogans.

What a port-side firm's site should list

  • What you haul, store or service, in the words shippers use.
  • Equipment, capacity and the area you cover.
  • Hours, including nights or weekends if you work them.
  • A quote form that emails every request straight to you.

What a shop on a Long Beach street needs

  • The street and district in the first lines of the page.
  • Hours, parking and whether you take walk-ins.
  • Photos of what you actually sell or make.
  • A phone number at the top that dials on one tap.

The shopping streets people name when they search

Long Beach runs business improvement districts along several of its commercial streets, and the city passes the assessments it collects back to each district's association. Here are streets a customer is likely to type.

  • Retro Row, 4th StreetVintage and collectible shops along East 4th Street. A boutique here should say "Retro Row" and "4th Street" in plain text, not only in a logo.
  • Belmont Shore, 2nd StreetA walkable run of boutiques, restaurants and nightlife near the water. On a busy street like this, parking tips earn their place on the page.
  • Bixby KnollsA north Long Beach district with its own business association and a strip of specialty shops.
  • East Village Arts DistrictThe downtown blocks between Alamitos Avenue and Long Beach Boulevard, known for galleries, boutiques and bars.
  • Cambodia Town, Anaheim StreetIn 2007 the city council gave the name Cambodia Town to a 1.2-mile business corridor on Anaheim Street between Atlantic and Junipero Avenues. The city is home to one of the largest Cambodian communities outside Southeast Asia.

Google says local results depend mostly on relevance, distance and prominence. Long Beach help that costs nothing

The Small Business Development Center hosted by Long Beach City College offers one-on-one advising at no charge, plus workshops on planning, finance and marketing. It belongs to the Los Angeles Regional SBDC network and is funded by the SBA and the state's GO-Biz office. The city's BizPort website, launched in October 2016, gathers the steps for a city business license and points owners to other programs. And the Long Beach Area Chamber of Commerce, which grew out of that port-minded Board of Trade, is another way to meet the owners and buyers around you.
