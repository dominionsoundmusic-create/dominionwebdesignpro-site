The post next door, under its restored name

Fort Hood covers roughly 214,000 acres right alongside the city. It carried the name Fort Cavazos from 2023 until the Army restored the Fort Hood name in 2025, now honoring Col. Robert B. Hood rather than the Confederate general it was first named for. Signs, maps and plenty of local websites still mix the two names. A page that uses the current one, and mentions the old one once for people who still search it, reads as up to date.

Killeen itself had 153,095 residents in the 2020 Census, and Census Bureau estimates show it has kept growing since. Texas A&M University-Central Texas, which started in 1999 as a Tarleton State branch, serves mostly non-traditional students. Central Texas College is in Killeen too. Both add to the stream of adults starting over in a new place.

Web design Killeen newcomers can read in thirty seconds

A family fresh off a move asks the same handful of things. Answer them on the first screen.

  • Where exactly are you? Name the gate or road you are near and the towns you serve: Killeen, Harker Heights, Copperas Cove, Nolanville.
  • Can I get in soon? Say whether you take walk-ins, how far out appointments run and how to book.
  • What does it cost? Prices or a clear way to ask. Military discounts, if you offer them, belong here in plain words.
  • Who am I dealing with? A short, honest about section with a real photo of you or your shop.

Gate-side storefronts and kitchen-table businesses

Two kinds of owner tend to need a Killeen site. The first runs a storefront competing with a dozen similar shops between the post and the highway, where small differences in a listing decide who gets the call. Google ranks local results by relevance, distance and prominence, so a page that states your services and the neighborhoods you cover gives it something accurate to work with. The second is a military spouse running a business from home who may move with the next set of orders. The SBA has pointed spouses to its Boots to Business training on installations. A site you own, rather than a rented builder page, can move with you.

Growth around the edges keeps the map changing. Nolanville, for one, has its own economic development corporation, which hired a director in 2022 to recruit businesses. Harker Heights and Copperas Cove keep growing too. If you serve those places, say so on the page.
