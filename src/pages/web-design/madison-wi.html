Saturday on the Square, and the other six days

The Dane County Farmers' Market sets up around the Capitol Square on Saturday mornings from April to November, and it is widely described as the largest producer-only farmers' market in the country. The rule is simple: everything for sale is grown, raised or made in Wisconsin by the person behind the stand. For 2026 the Saturday season runs April 11 to November 14, and a Wednesday market runs on Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard. Because vendors sell only what they produce, popular items can sell out before closing.

That last point is where a website earns its keep. A shopper who missed your eggs on Saturday wants to know how else to buy them. A farm or food maker's site can answer that the rest of the week.

  1. Where to find youWhich market days you attend, which side of the Square, and any weeks you skip.
  2. What is in seasonA short list you send over when it changes, updated as part of maintenance.
  3. How to buy between marketsCSA shares, farm pickup, restaurant and wholesale inquiries, each going to your inbox through the contact form.
  4. Who you areThe farm, the family, the town, and photos of the actual fields or kitchen.

A capital, a campus and a software giant next door

Madison had about 286,000 people in 2025, Wisconsin's second largest city after Milwaukee, in a metro area of about 710,000. Three big employers shape who your customers are.

State government

The Capitol sits in the middle of the Square, so downtown fills with government workers and visitors on weekdays. Lunch spots, printers and services within a short walk get searched by people who only have an hour.

UW-Madison

The university enrolled nearly 52,000 students in fall 2025, including an incoming class of 8,500. Newcomers know few neighborhood names, so they search by what they need and how close it is.

Epic in Verona

The health care software company employs around 13,000 people, most of them at its Verona campus. People who move for jobs like these still have to pick a dentist, a barber and a mechanic, and most start with a search.

Web design Madison WI neighborhoods make sense of

The east side has a long habit of backing its own shops. On Williamson Street, which locals call Willy Street, locally owned shops, restaurants and music venues have filled storefronts that once sat empty, and the American Planning Association credits neighbors with pushing back against chain development. The Willy Street Co-op grocery opened in 1974 and added a second store in Middleton in 2010. Atwood Avenue runs east from Williamson toward Schenk-Atwood, with its own row of local shops and cafes.

Customers in Madison talk in places like these: Willy Street, Atwood, Monroe Street, State Street, and suburbs such as Middleton and Fitchburg. If your shop is on one of those streets, the street name belongs in your page title and first paragraph, plainly.
