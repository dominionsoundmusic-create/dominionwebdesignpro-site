Where Waco does business

Waco sits on Interstate 35 between Dallas and Austin, which puts a steady stream of travelers on the highway past the city every day. Downtown and the area around the Silos draw most of the visitors. Across the Brazos River, East Waco's Elm Avenue reopened to traffic in September 2023 after roughly four years of reconstruction from MLK Boulevard to Spring Street, with new sidewalks, drainage and streetscape. Business owners there said the long closures hurt sales, and the reopening came with new hotels and renovated older buildings planned along the street.

Beyond downtown, the everyday economy looks like any growing Texas city: home services and contractors across Waco and the suburbs of Woodway, Hewitt and Robinson, medical and dental offices, auto shops, salons, restaurants, and businesses that serve the university.

What that means online is simple. Visitors do not know your street, so your site has to tell them clearly. Locals know the street but compare you with the shop across it, so your site has to give them a reason. And both groups are searching on a phone.

Baylor shapes the calendar more than outsiders expect. Move-in week, football Saturdays and commencement bring families who need a place to eat, a hotel nearby, a locksmith, a tire shop or a pharmacy, usually in a hurry and usually on a phone. A business near campus or along the highway that shows up clearly in those moments gets customers who may come back every semester for four years.

What visitors and locals look for on a Waco website

Three website problems that cost a Waco business customers

Hours that are wrong on the busiest days. Waco's calendar has peaks: Baylor move-in and graduation weekends, home football games, holiday shopping, and the steady flow of weekend visitors. A site that still shows last year's holiday hours, or no hours at all, sends a visitor to the next result. Updates are included with hosting and maintenance, so changing them is a quick email to Maurice, not a project.

A Facebook page doing a website's job. Plenty of good Waco businesses live entirely on social media. That works for regulars who already follow you. It does very little for someone typing your trade into Google from a hotel room off I-35, because Google has almost nothing to show them.

One page trying to serve everybody. A single home page that lists every service in a paragraph cannot match the specific searches people make. A page for each main service, and a page for each town you work in, gives each search a clear answer. That is the whole idea behind the service area page in Starter and the city pages in Pro.

Each of these is easy to check on your own site tonight. Open it on your phone, look up your hours, and search your trade plus "Waco" to see what a stranger would find.

None of these takes a large budget to fix. They take somebody sitting down to write the pages properly, which is the part of the job Maurice does by hand.
