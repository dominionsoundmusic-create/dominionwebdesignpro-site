The Golden Triangle, and the gusher that started it

On January 10, 1901, the Lucas well at Spindletop, on the south edge of Beaumont, threw oil more than 150 feet into the air and ran wild for nine days before the drillers capped it. Beaumont went from about 9,000 people in January to about 30,000 by March, and companies that grew into Gulf Oil and Texaco trace their start to that field.

More than a century later, Beaumont, Port Arthur and Orange still go by the Golden Triangle, and refining still drives the local economy. ExxonMobil's Beaumont refinery started up a $2 billion expansion in 2023 that added 250,000 barrels a day, taking it past 630,000 barrels a day and making it one of the largest refineries in the United States. The city itself had about 112,200 residents in the Census Bureau's 2023 estimate.

Turnaround weeks: what crews look up, and what your site should answer

A turnaround is a planned shutdown of a refinery or plant unit for inspection and repair. Major units go through one every three to five years, and the work takes so much specialized labor at once that refiners stagger them. For a small business near the plants, that can mean a wave of new faces who know nothing about town and search on a phone after a long shift.

Picture a family auto shop off the Eastex Freeway. A visiting pipefitter needs a tire fixed by morning and has never heard of the place. A neighbor who has brought her cars there for fifteen years wants to know if they are open Saturday. Both open the same website.

The answer is not two websites. It is a home page that leads with the quick facts a stranger needs, and service pages with the detail a regular appreciates. When a big job brings crews to town, a short notice at the top can say you are taking walk-ins early; when it ends, it comes down.

The same goes for where you are. Locals give directions by landmarks: Dowlen Road near Parkdale Mall, downtown along Calder Avenue, the Eastex Freeway heading north, the blocks around Lamar. Write those on your site in the words people say, and name Port Arthur, Nederland or Orange only if you truly serve them. Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence; an honest service area helps with the first two.

Boudin, crawfish and a border that blurs

Southeast Texas eats like its neighbor. Texas Highways describes Cajun food as everywhere in the region, from crawfish farms to boudin joints, and the Texas Almanac counts as many as 375,000 Cajuns living in Texas. Many families came over from Louisiana during the oil boom looking for steady work, the way fiddler Harry Choates's family did before he recorded "Jolie Blonde" in 1946.
