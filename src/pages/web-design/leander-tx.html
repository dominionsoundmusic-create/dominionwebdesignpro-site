A town that more than doubled in ten years

The U.S. Census Bureau counted 26,521 people in Leander in 2010 and 59,202 in 2020. Growth has kept going since, with Leander showing up again and again on lists of the fastest-growing cities in the country. Drive north on Ronald Reagan Boulevard or out toward the San Gabriel area and you can see why: new subdivisions, new schools and the shops that follow them.

For a local business, that kind of growth is a gift and a problem. The gift is a steady stream of households who need a dentist, a lawn service, a daycare and a pizza place. The problem is that every one of those households starts from zero. They do not ask the neighbors because they do not know the neighbors yet. They pick up a phone and search.

Where Leander's commerce is growing

Leander's commercial map has been redrawn by a toll road, a rail line and a deliberate push for a real downtown.

  • Toll 183AThe northern extension opened in 2012, running about five miles from RM 1431 in Cedar Park to just north of FM 2243 in Leander. Development around 183A and US 183 has picked up steadily since.
  • NorthlineA grocery-anchored project billed as Leander's first official downtown development, with about 17 acres of commercial and retail space along the 183A frontage.
  • Old TownThe original town center, where the city has offered incentives for downtown businesses and where the Leander Chamber of Commerce moved into its office on North West Drive in January 2026.
  • North LeanderAustin Community College's San Gabriel campus and St. David's medical development have pulled offices, clinics and services north.
  • The MetroRail stationLeander station, off US 183 just north of FM 2243, is the northern end of Capital Metro's MetroRail Red Line into downtown Austin.

If your business sits in one of these areas, say so on your site. "Two minutes off 183A at Hero Way" is something a customer can act on. "Serving Central Texas" is not.

Small business help close to home

The Leander Chamber of Commerce works with city staff on events meant to bring shoppers into town, publishes a community guide and runs the usual ribbon cuttings and networking. Joining early gets a new business in front of other owners who refer work to each other.

Outside the chamber, SCORE's Austin chapter offers free mentoring, and the Texas State University Small Business Development Center, which serves Williamson and Travis counties, provides no-cost or low-cost confidential counseling. Neither has anything to do with us. Both are free help worth taking.

And one more free thing that matters more than most owners realize: claim and fill in your Google Business Profile the week you open. It decides the map results, which most people see before they see any website.

An opening-month checklist for a new Leander business

If you are opening soon, this is the order that gets the most out of the least money. None of it needs an agency, and most of it is free.

  1. Claim your Google Business ProfilePick the most accurate category, add your hours, phone and service area, and upload real photos of the space or the work.
  2. Get the website live, even if it is smallFive clear pages beat a "coming soon" banner. Search engines need time to find you, so the clock should start now.
  3. Make your name, address and phone identical everywhereWebsite, Google, Facebook, the chamber directory. Small differences confuse both customers and search engines.
  4. Ask your first customers for reviewsFriends and family do not count, and Google can remove them. Real customers, asked politely, right after a job goes well.
  5. Join the chamber and show upRibbon cuttings and mixers are how other Leander owners learn you exist, and owners refer work to people they have met.

The website is step two, not step one, and it is the one we can help with.
