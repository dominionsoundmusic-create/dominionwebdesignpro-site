A city built on moving things and making things

"The Crossroads of America" has been Indiana's state motto since 1937, and Indianapolis adopted it as the city's slogan in 1988. It still fits. I-65, I-69, I-70 and I-74 all reach the city, and Indianapolis International Airport is home to FedEx's second-largest hub. The Indy Partnership, the region's economic development group, names advanced manufacturing and logistics among the region's core industries, with logistics concentrated in air transportation and in storage and fulfillment.

Big employers draw the headlines, but every warehouse and plant needs suppliers. Around them sits a ring of small companies: maintenance contractors, staffing firms, industrial cleaners, sign shops, electricians, trucking outfits, packaging suppliers, IT support shops. Indiana counted 569,851 small businesses in the SBA Office of Advocacy's 2024 profile, 99.4% of all businesses in the state. Many of them never sell a thing to the general public.

How a business buyer checks you out

Business buyers do most of their research alone. Gartner, the research firm, found that B2B buyers spend only about 17% of their buying time meeting with potential suppliers, and that time is split among every supplier they are considering. The rest goes to independent research, online and off, and to talking it over with their own team.

For a small supplier that is a real problem. The facilities manager comparing three commercial cleaners will look at your website, maybe forward it to a boss, and cut the list to two before picking up the phone. If your site is a single page with a logo and a phone number, you may never hear that you were considered.

What you do, in their words

Name the services and the industries you serve, using the terms buyers search for, not internal jargon.

Proof they can verify

License numbers, insurance, safety programs and the area you cover. Only what is true, stated precisely.

An easy next step

A quote request form with the fields you need, plus a named person and a number that is one tap away.

Indianapolis website design inside Unigov

Indianapolis has an unusual shape for a big city. Since January 1, 1970, the city and Marion County have operated as one consolidated government known as Unigov, created by the state legislature without a local referendum. A few places inside the county kept their own city or town government, including Beech Grove, Lawrence, Southport and Speedway.

For a business owner that has a practical effect on searches. An address can say Indianapolis while a buyer thinks of it as Castleton, Irvington or the Eastside, and suburbs just outside the county line, such as Carmel, Fishers and Greenwood, are separate cities that people search by name. A supplier who delivers across the region should have pages that name those places, not just the city.

For the storefronts in Indy's cultural districts

Not every Indianapolis client sells to other companies. In 2003 the city named its first cultural districts: Broad Ripple Village, the Canal and White River State Park, Fountain Square, Mass Ave and the Wholesale District. Indiana Avenue followed in 2004. The downtown districts are tied together by the Indianapolis Cultural Trail, and the Monon Trail runs north to Broad Ripple.

A gallery on Mass Ave, a bar in Fountain Square or a coffee shop in Broad Ripple lives on walk-in traffic and on people searching the district by name. Those sites need hours, a map, photos of the real space and the district named clearly. The work is different from a supplier's site, and the pages should be written differently.
