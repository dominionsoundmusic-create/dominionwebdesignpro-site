From pig iron to patient care

Sloss Furnaces made pig iron in Birmingham from 1882 until 1971. When it closed, preservationists fought off demolition, the city took over the site, and it became a National Historic Landmark. Now it is a museum of industry with a metal arts program and a concert stage. It is a fair symbol of how the local economy turned over.

The center of gravity today is the University of Alabama at Birmingham. UAB says it has some 28,000 employees across its university and hospital entities, and a 2023 economic impact study found UAB supported or sustained about 107,600 jobs in Alabama in 2022, roughly one in every 20 jobs in the state, with an annual impact of $12.1 billion. Jobs on that scale ripple outward into private practices, home care, medical billing, uniform shops, childcare, apartments and restaurants.

What people check on a health-related Birmingham site

The reader is often worried, busy or on a break between shifts. These are the pages that answer them fastest.

A family choosing home care

They want the counties and towns you cover, the kinds of care you give and do not give, how a first visit works, and a number that answers. Bury any of that and they call the next agency on the list.

A patient picking a practice

Accepted insurance in plain text, hours, parking, which providers see new patients, and how to book. A PDF menu of services nobody can read on a phone does not count.

A nurse or tech looking for shifts

Staffing firms need two front doors: one for the facilities that hire them and one for the workers they place. Each needs its own page with its own next step, not one muddled contact form.

Website design Birmingham AL owners need on both sides of Red Mountain

Birmingham customers describe where they live with a mix of neighborhood names and suburbs. Inside the city there are places like Avondale, Lakeview, Five Points South and Woodlawn, each with its own commercial strip. Lakeview and Avondale have seen new restaurants and shops in recent years, Avondale's 41st Street was picked for one of the city's first Complete Streets projects, and Woodlawn got its first new housing construction in more than 50 years with the Park at Wood Station townhomes.

South of Red Mountain are the communities locals call Over the Mountain: Homewood, Mountain Brook, Vestavia Hills and Hoover. They even have their own newspaper. A pediatric dentist in Vestavia Hills and a salon in Avondale are both "in Birmingham" to an outsider, but their customers search by the name they use at home.

That matters because Google says local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. If your site never says Homewood, it gives Google little reason to connect you with someone searching from Homewood. The fix is not a footer stuffed with town names. It is a page for each place you really serve, written about that place. Nobody can promise you a ranking, and we do not.

Startup help and business support in Birmingham

Innovation Depot

A startup center that opened in 2007 in a former Sears building downtown, in a facility of about 140,000 square feet, and works with UAB. It is often described as the largest tech incubator in the Southeast.

Greater Birmingham SBDC

Birmingham's Small Business Development Center sits inside Innovation Depot at 1500 1st Avenue North. It is part of the Alabama SBDC Network, hosted by the University of Alabama, which gives no-cost, confidential advising. UAB ran its own center until 2008, so older listings may still point there.

Birmingham Business Alliance

The region's business group runs small business programs aimed at what it calls the "missing middle," firms with 6 to 99 employees, including a 12-week intensive called Supplier Scale. In 2026, with Innovate Alabama, it awarded Match for Impact grants to 16 businesses, each receiving at least $40,000 in professional services.

Small Business Week

The Alliance also holds an annual Small Business Week; the 2026 edition ran May 3 to 9. It is a good place to meet the lenders, advisers and buyers who might later visit your website.
