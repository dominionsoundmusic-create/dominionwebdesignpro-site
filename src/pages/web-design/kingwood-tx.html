How Kingwood grew, and why it shapes your customers

Kingwood started in 1971 as a master-planned community, a joint venture between the Friendswood Development Company, then an Exxon subsidiary, and King Ranch. It was laid out as separate villages tied together by greenbelt trails under the pines, which is where the "Livable Forest" nickname comes from. Houston had annexed and later released parts of the area decades earlier; the 1990s annexation that brought all of Kingwood into the city was one of the most contested in Houston's history, and Texas changed its annexation law in 1999 partly because of it.

The result is a community of homeowners, many with families, spread across wooded neighborhoods with yards, pools and plenty of upkeep. That shapes the small businesses that do well there: lawn and tree care, pool service, roofing and air conditioning, pediatric dentists and orthodontists, tutoring, salons, veterinarians, and the restaurants clustered where people shop.

The City of Houston's super neighborhood profile names Kingwood's three main shopping districts: North Park Drive about a mile and a half east of US 59, Kingwood Drive about half a mile east of US 59, and Town Center at Kingwood Drive and West Lake Houston Parkway. If your storefront or service area sits along one of those, that belongs on your site in words a neighbor would use.
