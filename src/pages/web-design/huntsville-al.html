Why so many Huntsville firms sell to other firms

Redstone Arsenal is the center of gravity. The City of Huntsville puts the Arsenal's workforce at about 45,500 people, most of them federal civilians and contractor employees, with growth expected toward 50,000 as U.S. Space Command relocates there. NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center sits on the Arsenal too; in 2025 the Boston Globe reported more than 6,200 people working at Marshall, about 2,300 of them NASA civil servants and the rest largely contractors.

Next door is Cummings Research Park. Founded in 1962 and anchored by the University of Alabama in Huntsville, it covers 3,843 acres and is described as the second largest research park in the nation, after North Carolina's Research Triangle Park. The Encyclopedia of Alabama counts roughly 300 companies there with more than 26,000 employees, from defense and aerospace to biotech.

All of that spins off a long tail of small companies: machine shops, engineering consultancies, IT and cybersecurity outfits, technical writers, janitorial and facilities firms, electricians, staffing agencies, caterers who feed program reviews. Many never sell to the government directly. They sell to the primes who do, and those buyers check a vendor's website before the first call.

The pages a contracting officer looks for

None of these need jargon or a giant site. They need to be accurate, current and easy to find from the menu.

A capability statement page

The one-page summary buyers expect, usually also offered as a PDF of one or two pages. It covers your core competencies, what sets you apart, relevant past performance, and your company data: NAICS codes, UEI and CAGE code. There is no official government template, so clarity wins. We turn yours into a readable web page and link the PDF.

NAICS codes in plain view

NAICS, the North American Industry Classification System, is how buyers sort vendors by industry. List the codes you are registered under, with a sentence on what each one means for your work, so a buyer scanning for a specific code finds it in seconds.

Registration facts that match SAM.gov

SAM.gov registration is free and is required before a business can bid on federal contracts as a prime. It issues a 12-character Unique Entity ID, which replaced the old DUNS number in 2022, and it must be renewed every year. Practitioner guides warn that mismatches between your website, your capability statement and your SAM record raise questions, so we copy the exact legal name and identifiers you give us.

A clear "who we serve" page

Say which buyers you work with in general terms you are allowed to share: prime contractors, federal agencies, commercial customers, homeowners. Name past customers only where you have permission. A short, true page beats a wall of agency logos you cannot back up.

How a buyer checks you out before calling

Picture the process from the other side of the desk. A small business liaison needs three possible suppliers for a fabrication job. She searches SBA's Small Business Search, the tool that replaced the Dynamic Small Business Search in July 2025 and pulls company data from SAM.gov registrations. She finds your name, then opens your website. In under a minute she wants to confirm you are real, you do this kind of work, your codes match, and someone will answer the phone.

That last part matters more than it sounds. If your site has a contact form that goes nowhere or a number that rings out, she moves to the next name. Every site we build sends form leads straight to your email, and you can pair it with an AI phone agent that picks up when you are on a shop floor or in a SCIF with your phone in a locker.

Huntsville website design for a city that keeps growing

The 2020 Census made it official: Huntsville, with 215,006 residents, passed Birmingham to become Alabama's most populous city. The Census Bureau's estimate for July 2023 had Huntsville up to 225,564. Growth like that brings new households, new subdivisions and new customers who have never heard of your business, and they find local services the same way everyone does now, by searching.

Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. Distance means the town names on your site matter. Customers here think in places: Madison on the west side, Hampton Cove out past Monte Sano, Athens and Decatur across the county lines. A plumber or HVAC company serving all of those needs pages that say so, each written about that place, not one list of towns in the footer.

Inside the city, the old and the new sit side by side. Five Points, named for the meeting of Holmes Avenue, Pratt Avenue, California Street and Andrew Jackson Way, is a historic district built from the 1892 East Huntsville Addition. Lowe Mill, a 1900 cotton mill turned arts center that calls itself the largest privately owned arts facility in the country, holds 152 studios for more than 200 artists and makers. MidCity, on the old Madison Square Mall site at Research Park Boulevard and University Drive, is being rebuilt as a mixed-use district with shops, offices and housing. Each draws a different customer, and a downtown cafe or a maker selling at Lowe Mill needs a different site from a defense supplier.
