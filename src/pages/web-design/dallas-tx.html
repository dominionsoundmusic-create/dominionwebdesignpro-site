A city of distinct districts, and customers who search by them

Dallas is about 1.3 million people inside the city limits, according to the Census Bureau's 2023 estimate, and it does not behave like one market. Deep Ellum, east of downtown, is known for live music, bars and late-night food. Lower Greenville is a strip of restaurants and patios. Across the Trinity River in Oak Cliff, the Bishop Arts District is built around independently owned shops, galleries and cafes. Each of those places draws a different customer, and each is searched by name.

Language matters here too. Census estimates for 2019 to 2023 show 41.1 percent of Dallas residents five and up speak something other than English at home, nearly double the national rate. For a contractor, a clinic or a restaurant, that is a real question about who reads your site and how they decide to call.

The rest of the Dallas small business picture is what you would expect from a big service economy: trades and home services across the older neighborhoods and the suburbs, professional offices downtown and along the freeways, food businesses everywhere. What they share online is competition. A Dallas business needs pages that say exactly what it does and exactly where, or it gets lost behind the firms that do.

Before you sign with any Dallas web design company

Dallas owners who have been through a bad website project tend to describe the same few problems. Here is how to spot them before you pay, whoever you hire.

  • The site lives on their platform. If the agency's account holds your domain or your files, leaving can mean starting over. Ask who owns what, in writing.
  • The price grows after launch. A low build fee with a large monthly retainer can cost more in two years than a one-time build. Ask for the total for the first two years.
  • The pages are copies. Ask to see a city page from a site they built. If it reads like every other city page with the name swapped, yours will too.
  • Nobody answers after the sale. Ask who handles a change request and how fast. Here, it is Maurice, by phone or email.

Get the answers by email, not just on a call, so you can compare companies side by side later.

A good company will answer all four without hesitation. If the answers are vague, keep looking, and that includes looking at us.
