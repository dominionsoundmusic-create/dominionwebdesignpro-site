A city of eleven million first impressions a year

Scottsdale's own tourism study counted more than 11 million visitors in 2024: 4.9 million domestic overnight guests, 1.7 million international overnight guests and 5.1 million day-trippers. The city puts the economic impact at $3.7 billion, supporting more than 36,000 local jobs in hospitality and related work. Visitors do not stay inside the resorts, either. The same research found overnight visitors made 55% of their retail purchases at Scottsdale-based shops, and day-trippers 59%.

Hotels and resorts ran at about 75% occupancy from January through April in the 2024 to 2025 season, which the city calls its high season. Every one of those guests has a phone, and when they want a facial, a dinner table, a golf lesson or a gift, they search, look at two or three sites, and choose. A local customer living in North Scottsdale does the same thing, only with higher expectations, because they know the city.

Scottsdale website design for work people inspect up close

The businesses that lose the most to a weak site here are the ones whose service is personal: aestheticians and injectors, hair and nail studios, massage therapists, personal trainers, photographers, wedding and event planners, interior designers, and the trades who work inside expensive homes, from cabinetmakers to pool and landscape builders.

Their customers are looking for reasons to say no. Stock photos, a vague "luxury experience" headline, or a booking page that dead-ends will all do it. A plain, finished site with your real work, your real people and a clear next step does the opposite.

What "polished" means once you strip out the decoration

  • Your own photos of your own space and finished work, sized so they load quickly on a phone.
  • Who performs each service, with the license or training that applies to it stated plainly.
  • Starting prices or a price range, so the right clients self-select before they call.
  • One booking or inquiry path, repeated on every page, that works with a thumb.
  • A short, honest About page that sounds like the owner, not a brochure.
  • Your Google reviews shown on the site, if you have them (that is part of Pro).

Where Scottsdale customers actually are

Scottsdale is long and narrow, and the customer in Old Town is not the customer near the Airpark. A good site says which part of the city you serve and who you serve there.

Old Town

Tourism guides describe nine walkable districts inside about a square mile, among them Fifth Avenue's boutiques, the Arts District around Main Street and Marshall Way, where the Thursday evening ArtWalk has run since 1975, and the Scottsdale Waterfront along the Arizona Canal. Galleries, restaurants and boutiques here sell to visitors and locals in the same afternoon.

The Airpark

The city's Greater Airpark plan calls it the largest employment center in Scottsdale, and the city's airport page counts more than 2,500 small and medium-sized businesses and more than 48,000 workers around Scottsdale Airport. That is a weekday market for lunch spots, fitness studios, dry cleaners, IT help and anyone selling to offices.

The Shea corridor

The city has promoted the Cure Corridor, a health care and life sciences cluster along Shea Boulevard and Scottsdale Road that includes Mayo Clinic's campus on East Shea. Clinics, therapists and wellness businesses nearby compete for patients who research carefully.

Further north, resort country around Troon North and the golf communities brings a different buyer again. If your business serves one of these areas more than the others, the site should say so in plain words, because Google weighs how relevant you are to a search and how far you are from the person searching.
