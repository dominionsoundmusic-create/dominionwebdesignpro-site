One website design company, researched for each city

Plenty of US website design companies have a "locations" page with a few hundred city names on it. Click one and you get the same paragraph with the town swapped. Google has learned to ignore that, and so have the people searching.

The city pages listed here work differently. Each one started with research into that one place, then Maurice wrote the page from scratch. You will find the commercial roads that matter, the chamber of commerce and the free small business advising nearby, and a straight explanation of why local search works the way it does there. No page claims an office in a city where there is none.

The same method is what you buy for your own business. A city page on your site should read like it was written by somebody who looked at your town, because it was.

How each city page gets researched

The checklist below is the same one used for client service areas on the Pro and Agency packages.

  1. Search demand firstKeyword data shows whether people in that city actually search for the service, and how they phrase it. A town with no searches gets no page.
  2. The local business makeupWhat the economy runs on, from oil and medicine to tourism and military payrolls, and which kinds of small businesses grow around it.
  3. Real placesNeighborhoods and commercial corridors that a local would recognize, checked against city, chamber and news sources.
  4. Free local helpThe chamber of commerce, the Small Business Development Center, SCORE and city programs a new owner should know about.
  5. Sources on the pageEvery researched fact is linked at the bottom so anyone can check it. If a fact cannot be verified, it is left out.

The Texas and Georgia pages exist because those are the places researched so far. They are not a fence. A web design company that delivers remotely can serve a bakery in Vermont as easily as a roofer in Tyler, and the process is identical: you ask for the demo, you look at it, and you decide.

What changes for an out-of-state business is only the homework. Before writing a word, Maurice looks up your towns the same way the pages above were researched, so your service area pages talk about your streets and not about Texas.
