Why local search matters more as Plano gets better connected

In October 2025 DART opened the Silver Line, a 26-mile commuter rail line from Plano to DFW Airport, with Plano stations at 12th Street and Shiloh Road. The 12th Street station, one stop south of Downtown Plano, is also served by the Red Line and, at peak hours, the Orange Line. More ways in means more people passing through who did not grow up here and do not have a favorite plumber or a regular barber. When they need one, they search on a phone, and the businesses with a clear, fast website and a complete Google Business Profile get the call.

The same goes for new residents who move in for corporate jobs. They arrive without neighbors to ask. A website is often the first and only thing they check before they pick up the phone, so it needs to answer three questions in the first screen: what you do, which part of Plano and the surrounding suburbs you cover, and how to reach you right now.

What a Plano small business site should include

  • A plain statement of the service and the area, for example "Residential electrician serving Plano, Allen and Richardson."
  • One page per service, so a search for a specific job lands on the page that answers it.
  • Real photos of your work, your shop or your staff instead of stock pictures of handshakes.
  • A tap-to-call number at the top of every page and a contact form that has been tested.
  • Your hours, your map and your Google reviews where people can see them.
  • Page titles, descriptions and a sitemap set up so search engines can read the site properly.
