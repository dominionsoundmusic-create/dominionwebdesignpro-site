Web design Augusta GA owners can switch on for tournament week

Masters week has its own economy. Homeowners rent out their houses to visiting groups, and the practice is common enough that the federal tax provision letting a homeowner rent a home for up to 14 days a year without reporting the income is widely called the Augusta Rule. The Augusta Metro Chamber of Commerce runs the Masters Housing Bureau, which matches visiting groups with homes for the week.

For a small business, the week is short and loud. The site that serves your regulars may say nothing a visitor needs. Here is how the same business can talk to both groups.

An Army cyber town where buyers read before they call

The Army post outside the city has changed names twice since 2023. It was renamed Fort Eisenhower in October 2023, and in 2025 the Army restored the name Fort Gordon, this time honoring Master Sergeant Gary I. Gordon, who received the Medal of Honor for his actions in Mogadishu in 1993. It is home to U.S. Army Cyber Command, the Cyber Center of Excellence, the Signal Corps and the Georgia Cryptologic Center.

The state added to that in Augusta. The Georgia Cyber Center opened its first building, the Hull McKnight Building, on July 10, 2018, and a second building in January 2019, about 332,000 square feet in all on an Augusta University campus, with a cyber range, classrooms and a business incubator.

Small IT shops, managed service providers, trainers and consultants grow up around a base like that, and so do the firms that serve their staff. Their buyers tend to be careful readers. A site for that kind of company should have:

  • A services page that names what you actually do, in words a contracting or IT manager would search for.
  • A short about page with real names and a real way to reach a person.
  • Nothing that hints at sensitive work, locations or schedules. Less is safer here.
  • A fast, plain build with a contact form that goes to an inbox someone watches.
