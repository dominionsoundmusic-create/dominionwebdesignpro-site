This is a city where an art and design college fills the historic district with students, where galleries share blocks with coffee shops, and where half the owners you meet have a friend who "does websites." So we will not pretend design talent is scarce here. It is not.

What can be scarce is a price you know before you start, words written for your business, and someone who will still answer when something breaks next spring. Savannah website design when the town is full of designers

The Savannah College of Art and Design, known to everyone as SCAD, told WTOC that more than 15,000 students were enrolled when fall classes began in September 2025, with over 6,000 of them moving into residence halls that weekend. SCAD also runs an Atlanta campus, so not all of those students are in Savannah, but the effect on the city is easy to see. Graphic designers, illustrators, photographers and interactive design students are all around you.

For an owner, that is good news. You can hire young talent locally, and some of it is excellent. The trouble is rarely the design. It is everything around it: who writes the service descriptions, who sets up the page titles and sitemap so Google can read them, who hosts the site, and what happens when your designer graduates and moves to New York.

The squares, Starland and the names locals use

{{ img("images/cities/savannah-ga.jpg", "Savannah square with a tiered fountain, pink and green caladium beds and live oaks hung with Spanish moss in front of townhouses", 804, 1200, caption="Photo: Jrozwado, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.") }}

James Oglethorpe laid out Savannah's first four squares in 1733, and each new ward was built around one. Twenty-two of the original 24 squares survive, and the historic district became a National Historic Landmark District in 1966. Residents still give directions by them, which matters for a website: "two blocks off Chippewa Square" means more to a local than a street number.

South of the old city, the Starland District along Bull Street, roughly between Anderson Street and Victory Drive, has filled with independent shops, cafes and galleries. Its revival is usually traced to around 2000, when two SCAD students bought an empty dairy building on Bull Street.

Beyond the city line, customers say Pooler, Richmond Hill, Tybee Island or the Southside rather than "Savannah." The city itself had about 147,700 residents in the Census Bureau's 2023 estimate, while the metro area counted more than 400,000 people in the 2020 Census, so most of the market lives outside downtown.

Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. A page that truthfully names the areas you cover helps with the first two. Reviews and mentions around the web build the third over time.

Web design Savannah Georgia businesses need as the port and the plant grow

Tourism is the part of the economy everyone sees. The city estimated 12.9 million visitors in 2024, who spent about $4.1 billion, according to a 2026 City Council agenda item citing Visit Savannah. Restaurants, tour operators, inns and shops depend on being found on a phone by people who have never walked the street before.

The other part runs on trucks. The Port of Savannah handled 5.67 million twenty-foot equivalent units of containers in the fiscal year that ended June 30, 2026, just under the 5.7 million of the year before. Then there is the Hyundai Motor Group Metaplant America in Bryan County, which began building its first electric vehicles in October 2024 and held its grand opening in March 2025. In September 2025 Hyundai announced an expansion toward 500,000 vehicles a year by 2028, with 3,000 more workers, part of the roughly 8,500 jobs it has said the campus will eventually hold.

All of that means suppliers: electricians, welders, HVAC crews, cleaning companies, staffing agencies, food trucks that park by job sites, landlords renting to new workers. A purchasing manager at a warehouse or plant contractor will check your site before calling. They want to see what you do, the counties you cover, any safety or licensing details you can honestly list, and a phone number that gets answered. That kind of business-to-business site is less about style and more about clear, complete pages.
