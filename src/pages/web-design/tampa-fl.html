Two storms in two weeks, and what customers looked for

Hurricane Helene came ashore in Florida's Big Bend near Perry late on September 26, 2024, as a Category 4 storm. It never touched Tampa directly, yet preliminary data showed all eight valid tide gauges around Tampa Bay recording their highest water levels on record. St. Petersburg recorded more than six feet of storm surge, and low-lying Shore Acres flooded street by street. State crews shut the Gandy Bridge, the Howard Frankland, the Sunshine Skyway and the Courtney Campbell Causeway while the surge was up.

Thirteen days later, Hurricane Milton made landfall near Siesta Key on October 9 as a Category 3, about 70 miles south of Tampa. St. Petersburg logged more than 16 inches of rain, a flash flood emergency was declared for the bay area, and outages across Florida climbed past three million customers.

Think about what a customer needs in that week. Is the hardware store open, and until when? Does the vet in Seminole Heights have power? Is the roofer taking new inspections or booked solid? None of those answers live in a pretty homepage photo. They live in a line of text at the top of the page and in your Google hours, and both need to be easy to change while you are dealing with your own damage.

What a Tampa website needs for hurricane season

These are the pieces we plan into a Tampa Bay site from the first draft, not bolt on in October.

  • A notice bar you can change in one message. Closed, opening late, open with limited service, reopening Tuesday. One short line at the top of every page, and we update it for you the same day you send it if you would rather not log in.
  • Hours that match Google. Google lets you set special hours for short changes, up to six days in a row, and mark the business temporarily closed for longer shutdowns. Your site should say the same thing your profile says, or customers trust neither.
  • Pages that load on a weak signal. After a storm many people are on cell data with a dying battery. Small images, no autoplay video, and the phone number near the top.
  • A plain "after the storm" page for repair trades. What you inspect, whether you work with insurance paperwork, how to request a visit, and which parts of the bay you can reach right now.

Storm repair trades get searched in a hurry

When a tree comes through a lanai roof, nobody browses ten agency sites over coffee. They search "roof tarp Riverview" or "water damage Clearwater" on a phone, call the first two that look real, and hire whoever calls back. For roofers, tree crews, water mitigation companies, fence builders, pool screen repair and electricians, the website's job in that hour is short: prove you are local, prove you are working, and make the call button impossible to miss.

These are overwhelmingly small companies. Using Census business data, the SBA Office of Advocacy's 2025 Florida profile counts 63,945 small employers in the state's construction industry, 99.6% of construction employers, and small firms carry 82.5% of construction employment. A two-truck roofing outfit in Pasco is competing with national franchises that arrive after every storm, and its website is often the first place a homeowner decides whether it is the local crew or one more out-of-state name.

What a storm service page should say

Write it before the season starts, so it is ready to switch on. A useful page names the kinds of damage you handle, explains how you document it for an insurance claim, lists the counties and towns you serve, gives realistic response times, and tells people what to do in the meantime (photos, a tarp, shutting off water). It should not promise arrival times you cannot keep, and it should never pretend to be an emergency service if you are not one.

Tampa Bay is three cities and a lot of water between them

Outsiders say "Tampa." Locals know the bay splits the region. Tampa itself, with about 414,500 residents in the Census Bureau's 2024 estimate, sits in Hillsborough County. St. Petersburg and Clearwater are across Old Tampa Bay in Pinellas. North of both, Pasco County has grown fast, by well over 10% between 2020 and 2024 in both Census and state estimates, around places like Wesley Chapel. The Census metro area of Tampa, St. Petersburg and Clearwater counts about 3.4 million people. Three crossings tie Hillsborough to Pinellas: the Gandy Bridge, the Howard Frankland, which carries I-275 and is the busiest of the three, and the Courtney Campbell Causeway.

Those bridges shape how people search. A customer in Shore Acres will often look for someone on her side of the water, and a Brandon homeowner may never type "Tampa" at all. That is why web design in Tampa, and web design for Tampa Bay as a whole, has to deal in specific place names, not "serving the greater Tampa Bay area" pasted into a footer.

Inside the city

Ybor City grew from Vicente Martinez-Ybor's cigar factory, built in 1886, and Tampa annexed it the next year. It is now a National Historic Landmark District full of shops, restaurants and clubs. Seminole Heights, north of downtown, is a historic bungalow neighborhood whose Florida Avenue corridor is known for restaurants and craft breweries. South Tampa and Westshore, the city's big office district, round out the list most customers would name.

East and south Hillsborough

Brandon is an unincorporated community in east Hillsborough with no city government of its own, which is exactly why its residents search by the community name. Riverview, to the south, is much the same story for home services, family dentists and auto shops. A business based in either place usually wants a page for both.

Across the bridges and up north

Pinellas customers search St. Petersburg, Clearwater, Largo and the beach towns by name. Pasco customers search Wesley Chapel, Land O' Lakes and New Port Richey. A trade that crosses county lines needs a page per area it truly serves, each written about that place.

Web design Tampa Florida customers can find: how Google picks

Google says local results rest mostly on three things: relevance, meaning how well a listing matches what was searched; distance from the searcher or from the place named in the search; and prominence, or how well known the business is. Distance is the one you cannot fake, and on the bay it is stretched by water. A search for "AC repair Seminole Heights" leans on that name. If your site and your profile never mention Seminole Heights, you are leaving the match to luck.

So whichever website design company in Tampa you hire, ours or anyone's, the plan should start with a list: the neighborhoods and towns you actually work, ranked by how much of your business comes from each. Then each one gets real copy. We will not promise a ranking, because nobody honestly can. We will make your site clearly about your work in the places you name.

Local help for Tampa Bay owners, most of it free

Florida SBDC at USF

Part of Florida's Small Business Development Center network, hosted by the University of South Florida. It offers no-cost consulting and low-cost training to new and existing businesses across the Tampa Bay region.

SCORE Tampa

Volunteer mentors who meet with startups and established owners for free. The chapter's listed office is on East Palm Avenue in Tampa, and score.org can match you with a mentor by ZIP code.

City of Tampa Small Business Navigator

Run by the city's Economic Opportunity department since November 2023, the Navigator gives one-on-one guidance on city processes, resources and next steps, and points owners to partner programs for capital.

Tampa Bay Chamber

Also known as the Greater Tampa Chamber of Commerce. It reports more than 1,400 members, about 90% with 250 or fewer employees, and runs the Competitive Edge Series for small business members plus its Small Business of the Year awards.
