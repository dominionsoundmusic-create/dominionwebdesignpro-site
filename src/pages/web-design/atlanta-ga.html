Atlanta's small business economy runs on owners, not departments

LendingTree has ranked metro Atlanta first for its share of Black-owned businesses four years running, among the 100 metros with the most Black-owned businesses. In its latest study, which uses 2023 Census data, 10.7% of the metro's businesses with employees are Black-owned, against 3.4% nationally. Fayetteville, North Carolina and Washington, D.C. came next.

Those are businesses with payroll. Behind them sit many more one-person operations: barbers in the West End, caterers who work out of a shared kitchen, mobile detailers, braiders, tax preparers, personal trainers in Midtown. The Atlanta Wealth Building Initiative has reported a wide gap in what these businesses are worth: the average Black-owned business in Atlanta valued at about $58,085, against $658,264 for the average white-owned one. A business at that scale cannot carry a five-figure website and a monthly retainer, so the agencies never call, and the owner decides a real website is not for them. That is a pricing problem, not a quality problem.

Where a website matters most in Atlanta

Food is one of the most visible small business categories in the city: restaurants, catering, bakeries and food trucks from Old Fourth Ward to the Westside. These businesses live or die on being found at the moment someone is hungry and searching. Plenty of them run on an Instagram account and a phone number in a bio link. That works until a customer types "catering near me" into Google and finds someone else.

Health and personal care is just as big. LendingTree's study found that 26.2% of Black-owned businesses nationwide were in health care and social assistance in 2023: home care agencies, therapists, child care providers, medical billing. Those owners sell trust, and a prospective client checks the website before calling. A clear page that lists services, credentials you actually hold and how to book is the minimum.

Service trades come next. A plumber in Southwest Atlanta, a cleaning company covering Buckhead and Sandy Springs, a mobile mechanic working the I-285 loop: their customers search by job and neighborhood. A site with one page per service, the areas covered and a tap-to-call number at the top answers those searches in a way a social profile cannot.

Free help for Atlanta small businesses

  • Invest AtlantaThe city's development authority runs Small Business Resource Centers through its ATLinBusiness program, with no-cost one-on-one consulting, in person or online, for businesses inside the city of Atlanta.
  • UGA Small Business Development CenterThe University of Georgia's SBDC network gives no-cost consulting through metro Atlanta offices, including one at Morehouse College, and works with partners such as Georgia State University. Check its locations page for the office nearest you.
