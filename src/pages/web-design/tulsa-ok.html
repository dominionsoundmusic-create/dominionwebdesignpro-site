Greenwood, then and now

In the early 1920s the Greenwood District north of downtown was home to some 10,000 Black residents and a business strip with more than 70 businesses: hotels, grocery stores, restaurants, theaters, doctors' and lawyers' offices, two newspapers and a library. It was known nationally as Black Wall Street.

On May 31 and June 1, 1921, a white mob burned about 35 blocks of it, including more than 1,200 homes, its businesses, churches, a school and a hospital. Historians believe as many as 300 people were killed. In June 2021, on the centennial weekend, Greenwood Rising, a history center at the corner of Greenwood and Archer, was dedicated to tell that story in three galleries: before, during and after.

That history is a reason some Tulsa owners care who they hire. Dominion Web Design Pro is Black-owned, and more on that is on our Black-owned web design page. The terms are the same for every customer: free demo, hand-written pages, one-time price.

Tulsa Remote and a wave of new owners

Since late 2018, Tulsa Remote has offered a $10,000 grant, funded by the George Kaiser Family Foundation, to full-time remote workers and self-employed people from outside Oklahoma who move to Tulsa and stay at least a year. The grant is paid out over that year, and members also get coworking space and community events. The program says it has welcomed more than 4,000 people.

Many newcomers keep their remote jobs. Some start side businesses, consulting practices, studios or shops. They also become customers who do not know which plumber everyone's parents used, so they search. For a long-standing Tulsa business, that is the reason a current website matters: the new neighbor on your block will find you online or not at all.

Name the district, not just the city

Tulsans describe where they are by district, and a site should too. Downtown's Deco District, roughly 1st to 8th Streets between Denver and Cincinnati Avenues, holds Art Deco buildings from the 1920s and 1930s oil boom. The Pearl District leans toward music, cafes and brewpubs. Cherry Street is known for restaurants, boutiques and antiques. Brookside runs along South Peoria Avenue, and Kendall-Whittier has a reputation for makers and murals.

Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. Saying "a frame shop on Cherry Street" or "HVAC service for midtown and Brookside" helps with the first two in a way that "serving the greater Tulsa area" never will.

Web development in Tulsa: what you probably need, and what you do not

"Web development Tulsa" is a common search, and it often comes from an owner who is not sure what to ask for. Before you hire a web designer in Tulsa or a developer, here is a quick way to sort out which one you need.

Most small businesses need

  • Pages that say what you sell, where and to whom
  • A phone link and a contact form that reaches you
  • Fast loading on a phone
  • Titles, descriptions and a sitemap Google can read
  • Someone to update it when hours or prices change

Custom development is for

  • Customer logins and accounts
  • Online stores with carts and inventory
  • Booking systems built from scratch
  • Connecting to your own business software
  • Apps that do work, not just explain it
