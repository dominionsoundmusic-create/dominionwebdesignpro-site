Chattanooga website design starts with a fact most cities cannot claim: the local power company wired the whole city with fiber and began offering gigabit internet in 2010, which is how Chattanooga got the nickname Gig City. That speed is a real advantage for anyone running a business from home or an office here. It also hides a trap. When your own connection is that fast, a bloated website feels fine on your screen and loads slowly for the customer who matters.

Dominion Web Design Pro builds light, hand-written sites for Chattanooga shops, trades and young companies. Maurice Johnson writes and builds each one from Big Sandy in East Texas, and you see a free demo before you pay anything.

How a power company ended up selling gigabit internet

The fiber was not built for internet at first. In the mid-2000s EPB, the city's electric utility, decided to build a smart grid, and the grid needed a fiber backbone so devices along the lines could talk to each other. About $111 million in federal stimulus money let EPB finish the job in roughly two years instead of five. The network was unveiled in September 2010 with gigabit access for EPB power customers across its area, and EPB is described as the first company in the United States to offer 1-gigabit internet over fiber.

Early sign-ups were slow. The fastest tier started around $350 a month, and Ken Hays of The Enterprise Center later recalled that there were very few takers at first. In October 2015 EPB went further and launched community-wide 10-gigabit service. VentureBeat later described how the city used the fiber to build up its tech community.

Fast lines on your end, ordinary phones on theirs

Your customer is not on your connection. A homeowner on the North Shore looking for a roofer, a buyer comparing you to a competitor in another state, a tourist downtown on a weekend: they are on mobile data or hotel Wi-Fi. A home page carrying ten full-size photos and a stack of tracking scripts can take long enough to load that they give up.

So we build the other way around. Every photo is saved in several sizes and the phone downloads the small one. The main image loads first and the rest load as you scroll. Fonts are hosted on the site itself. None of that is exotic, and it is the part of a website most owners never see.

  • Check whether your phone number is real text you can tap, or part of an image.

A founder's first website: what it needs now and what can wait

Gig City attracts people starting something. The temptation is to build everything at once. Most first sites do better small and clear.

Needs it on day one

  • One sentence on what you make and who it is for, above the fold.
  • Real names and faces behind the company.
  • A way to request a demo, a quote or a call that lands in your inbox.
  • Titles, descriptions and a sitemap so search engines can read it.

Can wait until customers ask

  • A blog you will not have time to feed.
  • Pricing tiers before you know your pricing.
  • Animation that slows the page down.
  • Pages for markets you have not entered yet.

Chattanooga web design for shops and trades, block by block

Most businesses here are not startups. They are mechanics, agents, contractors, cafes and salons, and their customers search by the part of town they know: downtown along the riverfront, the Southside, the North Shore, Main Street, up on Lookout Mountain. A page that names the neighborhoods you serve, says how to reach you and shows current hours answers the search the way a person would.

Relevance is the part a site controls now, by plainly naming what you do and where.
