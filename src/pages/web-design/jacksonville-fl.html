Jacksonville website design for a city you cannot cross on a lunch break

People often call Jacksonville the biggest city in the lower 48 by area. A Jacksonville Today fact-check found that claim holds for total area, water included, while Greeley County, Kansas, which merged with the city of Tribune in 2009, has slightly more land. Either way, the scale is the point. On a city this size, the word "Jacksonville" on its own tells a customer and Google very little about where you actually work.

Think about an air conditioning company based on the Westside. Its crew can reach Orange Park in Clay County quickly, but a call from Ponte Vedra might mean a long drive each way. If the website lists every corner of the city equally, the owner pays for that in wasted trips and unhappy callers.

A better site says where you go, how far, and whether there is a trip charge outside that zone. It might have one page for the Westside and Orange Park and another for the Southside, each written about that area, so the right customer finds the right page.

Navy families find you from scratch

Jacksonville is a Navy town. The City of Jacksonville lists NAS Jacksonville, Naval Station Mayport and nearby Naval Submarine Base Kings Bay in Georgia among the installations employing thousands of active duty, reserve and civilian workers. Citing a January 2024 Florida summary, the city puts Duval County's military-related economic impact at about $9.1 billion, roughly 17 percent of the county economy, with about 68,060 jobs. Local estimates, including a U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development market study, have long put the number of people leaving the military who settle in the area each year at around 3,000.

Families on orders arrive without a neighbor to ask for a dentist, a pet sitter, a mechanic or a moving cleaner. They search on a phone, often before the moving truck shows up. A site that serves them well does a few simple things:

  • Says which bases and parts of town you are near, in plain words.
  • Explains how to book and what a first visit costs, so a newcomer can decide without a phone call.
  • Mentions any military discount you really offer, and leaves it out if you do not.
  • Loads fast and reads well on a phone, because that is where the search happens.

Two programs worth a Jacksonville owner's time

Florida SBDC at UNF

The Florida Small Business Development Center at the University of North Florida offers consulting by appointment at little or no cost, paid for by federal SBA funds and contributions from the university, the City of Jacksonville and other partners. It serves 18 North Florida counties, including Duval, Clay, Nassau and St. Johns, with satellite centers on the Northside and Westside.

JSEB certification

The city's Jacksonville Small and Emerging Business program certifies small firms for city contracting. The business must be in Duval County and its owners must live in Duval, Baker, Clay, Nassau or St. Johns County, along with rules on net worth, ownership and control. The city holds JSEB certification training on the second Tuesday of each month.
