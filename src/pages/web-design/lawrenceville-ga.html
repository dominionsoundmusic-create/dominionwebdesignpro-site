Gwinnett customers do not all search in the same language

Gwinnett is one of the most diverse counties in the country; Bisnow, reporting on the 2020 census diversity index, placed it eighth nationally. Pleasant Hill Road in Duluth has long been known as a hub for the region's Korean-owned businesses, and Latino, Vietnamese, Chinese, Indian and African American owners run businesses along corridors across the county.

For a business owner, that has a consequence most website advice ignores. A restaurant on Highway 120 might be found by someone typing in English on Monday and in Korean on Tuesday. A contractor in Norcross may get referrals in Spanish and online leads in English. The right answer is rarely a full second website. It is a few decisions made on purpose: which page, if any, gets a second language, whether your business name appears in both scripts, and whether the phone number is one tap away everywhere.

What different Lawrenceville businesses need from a site

Restaurants and shops near the square

Hours that are actually current, a menu or product list that loads as text rather than a blurry photo of a printed sheet, parking directions, and a map. Downtown events send people searching for dinner nearby; the page has to answer before they walk past.

Contractors and home services

A page per job type, the Gwinnett cities you cover, photos of finished work, and a form that asks for the address and the problem so you can quote the right job. Homeowners in this county compare several companies before calling one.

Clinics and family practices

Services, insurance notes, languages spoken by the staff, and a booking or call button that works with one thumb. For a parent new to the area, the languages line alone can decide the call.

Professional offices

Accountants, insurance agents and title companies need a calm, credible site with real names and photos, clear service lists and a simple way to send documents or book a meeting.
