A port city where the buyer is often a plant

The Port of Corpus Christi is the leading crude oil export port in the United States, and its chief executive has said roughly 60% of the crude the country exports leaves through it, about 2.3 million barrels a day. In 2025 the Corpus Christi Ship Channel handled 203.4 million tons of cargo, down 1.5% from the year before as crude eased and liquefied natural gas kept climbing. Refineries and plants sit along that channel, and Texas A&M's real estate research center counts nearly $60 billion of private investment in petrochemical and manufacturing sites near the port from 2012 to 2023.

The water shapes daily traffic too. The new Harbor Bridge, which opened in late June 2025 after nearly a decade of work, was built higher over the channel so bigger ships can reach the port, and it is described as the longest cable-stayed bridge in the country. For a small contractor, all of this adds up to steady demand from big sites that are careful about whom they let through the gate.

What a plant buyer looks for on a contractor's site

Federal rules push large facilities to be choosy. Under OSHA's process safety management standard, an employer that brings in contractors for work in covered processes has to get and evaluate information about each contract employer's safety performance and programs when choosing one, and keep evaluating them afterward. Paperwork and prequalification handle the formal part. Your website is often the first look, so it should answer the obvious questions before anyone has to ask:

  • Your crafts and services, in their words. Pipe welding, structural fabrication, hydro blasting, insulation, scaffolding, crane and lift rental, confined space training. Name each one you do and stop there.
  • Your safety program in plain English. What training your crews carry, how you run job safety briefings, who your safety contact is. Only list what you can back up with documents.
  • Equipment and crew size. A short, true list helps a superintendent judge whether you fit a turnaround or a small repair job.
  • Where you will work. Corpus Christi, Portland, Ingleside, Robstown, Aransas Pass, or further up the coast. Say it outright.
  • A downloadable capability statement. One page a buyer can forward to a manager, with the same facts as the site.
  • A name and a number. The person who quotes, and how to reach someone when a unit goes down at 2 a.m.

Notice what is not on that list: big claims. A buyer who has read a hundred vendor sites trusts specifics far more than adjectives, and we write that way on purpose.

The island trade, briefly

East of the city, Padre Island National Seashore protects what the National Park Service calls the largest stretch of undeveloped barrier island in the United States. Port Aransas, on the north end of Mustang Island, markets itself on 18 miles of continuous beach, and a free state ferry links it to the mainland. Businesses out there sell to people who arrived an hour ago and are planning on a phone in a parking lot. A charter captain, a bait shop or a beach rental needs a site that loads fast on a weak signal, shows the meeting spot on a map and takes a booking request in a few taps. Everything else can wait further down the page.

A made-up example: a small fabrication shop in Calallen

Picture a six-person shop off the highway that does structural steel and pipe spools. Today its online presence is a map listing and a cousin's old site with a dead contact form. Work comes by word of mouth, and the owner loses bids to bigger outfits because a new buyer cannot find anything to forward upstairs.

The site we would write for a shop like that is short and specific: a home page that says what they fabricate and for whom, a services page per craft, a safety page in the owner's own words, a page about the area they cover, and a contact page with a quote form that goes straight to the estimator's inbox. Starter covers those five pages. If the shop wants pages for Portland, Ingleside and Robstown, each written about that town, Pro is the better fit.
