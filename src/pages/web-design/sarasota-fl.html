Web design Sarasota Florida businesses need starts with one plain fact about the people reading it. In Sarasota County, a large share of customers are retirees, and plenty of them are reading your site on a phone with the text size turned up. If your page breaks when that happens, or hides the phone number in a pale gray footer, you lose the very customer with the time and the money to come in.

Dominion Web Design Pro is Maurice Johnson. He writes every page himself, shows you a free demo of your own site before you spend a dollar, and charges one printed price.

Web design Sarasota Florida needs when more than a third of the county is over 65

Census Bureau estimates reported by Sarasota Magazine put 38.8% of Sarasota County residents at 65 or older in 2025. Inside the city limits the share is lower, 29.3% in the Census Bureau's QuickFacts figures, but the county is where most of your customers live.

None of that means old-fashioned design. It means design that does not make anyone work. Here is how that turns into choices on the page:

What a reader needsHow the page handles it
Text that is readable without zoomingLarge body type, short lines, dark text on a white background, no thin decorative fonts for anything important.
A clear way to reach youThe phone number at the top of every page, written out and tappable, plus a button that says "Call" in words.
Confidence about where you areYour street address, the nearest landmark road, and parking notes written in plain sentences.
No surprisesNo pop-ups covering the page, no auto-playing video, no menus that only open on hover.
Room for a bigger text settingLayouts that reflow when someone turns up the phone's font size, instead of overlapping or cutting words off.

How we check a Sarasota page before you see it

  1. Read it on a small phoneEvery page gets looked at on a narrow screen first, because that is where small type and crowded buttons show up.
  2. Turn the text size upWe enlarge the font and make sure nothing overlaps, disappears or forces sideways scrolling.
  3. Find the phone number in two secondsIf it takes longer than that from any page, the layout changes.
  4. Read the words out loudShort sentences and plain names for your services, so a first-time visitor understands what you do without a glossary.

A dining room, a menu and a reservation line

Restaurants show the problem most clearly. A menu posted as a photo of a printed page is unreadable on a phone and invisible to Google. Put the menu on the page as real text, list the hours for each day, say whether you take reservations and how, and put the number where a thumb can reach it. A couple planning dinner near Main Street or St. Armands Circle should be able to decide in under a minute.

The same goes for shops on Siesta Key or in Gulf Gate and service businesses down in Venice: say where you are in the words customers use, and say it near the top.
