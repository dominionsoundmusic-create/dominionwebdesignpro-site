Web design in McDonough, GA starts on the square

The blocks around the Henry County Courthouse, a building from 1897 that is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, still hold the kind of businesses a courthouse town always collects: law offices, title and insurance agents, cafes, salons, boutiques and the lunch places that fill up when court is in session. Each May the square and the surrounding streets fill for the Geranium Festival, an arts and crafts show with more than 400 booth spaces. It started in 1977 as the Henry County Chamber Arts and Crafts Festival, moved to the square in 1979 and has been run by the McDonough Lions Club since 1980, with booth money going to Henry County charities and to help the visually impaired. 2026 marked its 49th year.

A festival weekend is the clearest test of a downtown website. Thousands of visitors walk past your door, and some of them look you up later that evening on a phone. If your site shows last year's hours, a menu as a blurry photo or no map at all, that second visit never happens. The square rewards businesses whose pages answer three questions fast: what you sell, when you are open and where to park.

Three exits, three different customers

Most of McDonough's newer commerce sits along I-75. Each exit pulls a different kind of search, and a site written for one of them can miss the others.

Exit 221, Jonesboro Road

The interchange next to Henry Town Center and the road west toward Lovejoy. Henry County is using local transportation sales tax money to widen Jonesboro Road between I-75 and North Mount Carmel Road, which means detours for a while. Restaurants and service shops along it need current directions on their sites.

Exit 218, Highway 20 and 81

The corridor toward Hampton, with retail on SR 20 and industrial parkways running beside the interstate. Auto service, quick lunch spots and suppliers here are chosen in a hurry, often from a map result.

Exit 216, Highway 155

Where the I-75 South Metro Express Lanes begin. Commuters heading north toward Atlanta pass it every morning, so businesses near it are found by people searching on the move.

The logistics side of the county keeps growing too. Highline Warren announced a $170 million distribution center at 830 Highway 42 South, in a 1.1 million square foot building, with about 160 jobs and operations expected to start in late 2026. Every new warehouse brings shift workers who need lunch, childcare, car repair, barbers and apartments, and most of them find those by searching from the parking lot.
