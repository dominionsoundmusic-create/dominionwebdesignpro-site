Ask someone from Mesa where they live and they will not say Phoenix. Good website design Mesa owners can use starts there: a site that talks about Mesa streets, Mesa neighborhoods and the people who shop on them, instead of a generic page about "the Valley." The city describes itself as the third largest in Arizona and second largest in the metro, with more people than Atlanta or Miami, and the Census Bureau estimated 517,151 residents in July 2024.

Dominion Web Design Pro is Maurice Johnson, who writes each page himself, builds a free demo first and charges a printed, one-time price.

Four parts of Mesa, four different customers

Downtown on Main Street

Valley Metro's light rail extension to Gilbert Road and Main Street opened in May 2019. The three years of construction before it were hard on downtown businesses, and some cut their hours to get through. Today the train brings riders past shops, cafes and offices that want foot traffic from people who are not from the neighborhood.

The Asian District on Dobson Road

In 2020 the city named the stretch of Dobson Road between Main Street and Broadway Road the Asian District, and the light rail stop now carries the name. The city's own development site counts nearly 100 restaurants, specialty markets and service businesses along a two-mile stretch.

Falcon Field and north Mesa

Falcon Field opened on September 14, 1941 as the No. 4 British Flying Training School, where thousands of Royal Air Force cadets learned to fly; two hangars from that era still stand. Today the city runs it as a working airport, surrounded by north Mesa neighborhoods and the service businesses that keep them going.

The Gateway area in the southeast

The city markets the Mesa Gateway Area as a developing 35-square-mile district around Phoenix-Mesa Gateway Airport, with aerospace, data centers and manufacturing nearby and the Eastmark master-planned community growing beside it. New households there need everything from dentists to pest control.

A Mesa business should say which of these it serves. Google says local results weigh relevance, distance and prominence, so a family dentist near Eastmark and a pho shop on Dobson are answering different searches, from different people, a few miles apart.

Mesa websites for neighborhood practices

A lot of Mesa's small businesses are practices that live on repeat visits: family dentists, veterinarians, chiropractors, physical therapists, tutoring centers, hair salons. Their customers are local families, and they choose with a few questions in mind. Are you taking new patients? Do you take my insurance? Are you open Saturday? Can I park easily?

A good site answers those on the first screen and keeps them current. That is less glamorous than a new logo, and it is what fills the calendar. Maurice writes each service page around the questions your front desk answers every day, so the site does some of that answering for you.

For a restaurant or grocery in the Asian District, the site has a narrow job: the menu as readable text, the hours, the address with parking notes, and a way to order or call. Photos of real dishes help more than anything else. If your customers search in Vietnamese, Chinese, Korean or another language, pages in that language should be written or checked by a fluent speaker; we do not write them, and we would rather say so than hand you a machine translation.

Web design in Arizona, and why your page should still say Mesa

Plenty of owners search for "web design in Arizona," "web design AZ" or "web design Mesa AZ" because they are not sure who to look for. That is a big pool: the SBA Office of Advocacy's 2024 profile counted 678,357 small businesses in Arizona, 99.5% of all businesses in the state, employing about 42.8% of its workers. Whoever you hire, the site itself should be local. A statewide page competes with everybody; a page about your trade in Mesa competes with the handful of businesses that actually serve your customers.

The same goes for clinics. A veterinary or medical office near Falcon Field should say north Mesa, list the nearby neighborhoods it sees patients from, and explain after-hours care in plain words.

Mesa's own help for small businesses

  • Mesa Business Builder. The City of Mesa Office of Economic Development runs MesaBusinessBuilder.com as an online catalog for licensing, permits, financing and consulting. Its Small Business Assistance Program has offered free one-on-one consulting to businesses with a Mesa business license and fewer than 25 full-time employees, and its downtown space, MBB @ The Studios, opened in May 2024 with free training rooms and a podcast studio.
  • Mesa Chamber of Commerce. For more than a century it has advocated for Mesa businesses, and it runs networking, education and a member podcast from downtown on Center Street.
  • Maricopa SBDC. The Small Business Development Center led by Maricopa Community Colleges gives no-cost advising and mostly free workshops across the county.
