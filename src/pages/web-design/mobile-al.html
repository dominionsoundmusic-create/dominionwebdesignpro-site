Three big buyers on the bay

A lot of small firms in Mobile sell to one of these, or to someone who does.

  • The shipyardAustal USA has built ships in Mobile since setting up there in 1999, including aluminum warships for the U.S. Navy such as the littoral combat ship USS Independence. A yard that size buys fabrication, electrical work, tooling, cleaning, training and staffing.
  • The flight lineAirbus picked the Brookley Aeroplex for an A320 family final assembly line in 2012 and opened a second line there for the A220 in May 2020. In July 2025 it delivered the 100th A220 built in Mobile.
  • The waterfrontThe Alabama Port Authority has estimated that the Port of Mobile contributed $89.1 billion in economic value to the state in 2024 and is tied, directly or indirectly, to about one in seven Alabama jobs. A $131 million container berth is planned with APM Terminals, with work slated to start in 2026.

What a purchasing agent reads before calling a Mobile vendor

Large buyers rarely say so out loud, but they screen suppliers on the web first. The questions are dull and practical, and a short site that answers them beats a flashy one that does not.

The buyer wants to knowWhere your site answers it
What exactly do you make, fix or supply?A services page with one plain section per kind of work, written in the words a buyer types, not a slogan
Can you handle a job our size?An about page with your equipment, shop space, crew size and service radius, stated honestly
Are you licensed, insured and current?License numbers and insurance in text on the page, where a buyer can copy them
Who picks up when we call?A phone number that taps to call on every page, and a form that lands in your inbox

We will not write that you supply a company you do not supply, hold a certification you do not hold, or have done work you have not done. Name a customer on your site only when you have their permission. A true page with no logos is worth more to a careful buyer than a wall of borrowed ones.

Web design Mobile AL customers find by neighborhood and by shore

Ask someone where a business is in Mobile and you get a place name, not a ZIP code. Downtown, Lower Dauphin Street (locals say LoDa) is the walkable stretch of bars, restaurants and music venues, and on the second Friday of each month part of it closes to cars for Artwalk. Just west, Old Dauphin Way is the largest historic district in the city, 766 acres of mostly working-class frame houses with bigger homes on the main streets, the kind of area where a painter, a roofer or a plumber gets steady calls. Farther out, Airport Boulevard carries shoppers past the former Bel Air Mall, now the Shoppes at Bel Air.

Across the bay, the Eastern Shore is its own market. Daphne, Fairhope and Spanish Fort share a chamber of commerce, and plenty of their residents would rather hire someone on their side of the water. Add carnival season, which Mobile's city government calls the oldest verified organized Mardi Gras in the United States, dating to 1703, and you have weeks when hours, parking and road closures change for every shop near a parade route.

Google says local results lean on relevance, distance and prominence. If your site never mentions Fairhope, it gives Google little reason to show you to someone searching there. The answer is a real page for each place you serve, written about that place, not a footer crammed with town names. No one can promise a ranking, and we do not.

A city called Mobile, and a site that works on one

The joke writes itself, but the point is serious. A tow truck driver, a ship superintendent and a homeowner with a leaking water heater are often standing up when they search. Here is what we build into every page so the phone version does its job:

  • The number up top. Tap to call in the header of every page, not buried at the bottom.
  • Text you can read without pinching. Short paragraphs, real headings and buttons big enough for a thumb.
  • Photos that load fast. Images sized for each screen, so a site does not crawl on a weak signal near the docks.
  • Hours and areas in words. Typed out on the page, so they show up in search and nobody has to open a PDF.

Free business help on both sides of Mobile Bay

Alabama SBDC at South Alabama

The University of South Alabama hosts the regional office of the Alabama Small Business Development Center Network in Mobile. Advising is confidential and free for Alabama owners and covers a six-county area. The statewide network is hosted by the University of Alabama.

Mobile Area Chamber of Commerce

The chamber's business development navigator gives free counseling on planning, launching or growing a company, and chamber membership is not required. It also runs a nine-week Minority Business Accelerator for companies getting ready to scale.

Eastern Shore Chamber of Commerce

Serves Daphne, Fairhope and Spanish Fort from an office on Fairhope Avenue. It began in 1924 as the Fairhope Chamber of Commerce, and the Alabama House marked its 100th year in 2024.
