Web design Raleigh North Carolina owners can rely on has to deal with one fact first: most of your customers do not live in Raleigh. They live in Cary, Apex, Garner, Wake Forest, Morrisville or Fuquay-Varina, and they type their own town into Google. A site that only says "serving the Triangle" leaves most of those searches on the table.

Dominion Web Design Pro is Maurice Johnson. He writes each page by hand for one business, shows the owner a free demo before any invoice, and charges a printed price that is paid once.

Web design Raleigh North Carolina customers find: twelve towns, twelve names

Wake County has twelve municipalities: Apex, Cary, Fuquay-Varina, Garner, Holly Springs, Knightdale, Morrisville, Raleigh, Rolesville, Wake Forest, Wendell and Zebulon. Durham and Angier also reach over the county line. Each has its own town hall, its own downtown or main road and, for search, its own name. A homeowner in Rolesville looking for a gutter cleaner types "Rolesville", not "Wake County".

Google says local results are ranked by relevance, distance and prominence. A truthful page about the work you do in Apex helps with the first of those. Nobody can move your shop closer to the searcher, but you can stop leaving the town name out.

A Garner plumber, walked through

Picture a two-truck plumbing company based in Garner that takes calls from Raleigh, Fuquay-Varina and Holly Springs. Its old site has one page that lists "Raleigh and surrounding areas." When someone in Holly Springs searches for a water heater repair, that page gives Google nothing to match.

The better build is a home page for the business, a page for each service it actually sells, and a page for each town where it has trucks most weeks. Each town page says what is true there: typical drive time, the kinds of homes it services, how to book. No town page is a copy of another with the name swapped.

That is the kind of site we write. The plumber is an example, not a customer; the method works the same for a cleaner, a landscaper or a pet sitter.

Inside the Raleigh city limits

Raleigh itself is a set of districts with their own crowds. The Downtown Raleigh Alliance describes five core downtown districts: Fayetteville Street, Moore Square, Glenwood South, the Warehouse District and the Capital District, home to the State Capitol, the Legislative Building and the Governor's Mansion. Glenwood South started as a strip of warehouses and art supply shops and became a restaurant and retail center.

North of downtown, Kane Realty bought the old North Hills Mall in 2001 and rebuilt the area as a walkable mixed-use district; the company credits it with creating Raleigh's Midtown. A salon in Glenwood South, a boutique at North Hills and a caterer near Moore Square each draw from a different slice of the city, and each site should say where it is in words a local would use.

Selling to the state, to RTP and to the people who work there

Raleigh is the state capital, so agencies, associations and the firms that serve them are part of daily business. Research Triangle Park sits between Raleigh and Durham: about 7,000 acres, roughly 78% in Durham County and 22% in Wake, with more than 385 companies and about 55,000 employees, according to the Research Triangle Foundation.

That gives a Raleigh owner two kinds of buyer. A bookkeeper, IT consultant or commercial cleaner selling to offices needs a site that answers the questions a purchasing contact asks: what you do, who you have the insurance and paperwork for, how fast you reply. A food truck or a car detailer serving the people who work in those offices needs hours, location and a phone number at the top.

Free and low-cost help for Wake County owners

  • The SBTDC office at NC State serves Wake and eight nearby counties (Franklin, Granville, Halifax, Johnston, Nash, Northampton, Vance and Warren). It is one of 16 SBTDC offices across North Carolina.
  • The City of Raleigh's Building Up-fit Grant can match up to 50% of qualified interior construction costs for businesses with 49 or fewer employees inside city limits, with applications taken in set windows during the year and paid after the work is finished.
  • The City's Office of Economic Development and Innovation also funds community groups that support underrepresented entrepreneurs through its Impact Partner Grant.
  • The Greater Raleigh Chamber runs a Member Services and Small Business department and has hosted a quarterly roundtable that brings city, county, SBA and nonprofit lenders together to share resources.
