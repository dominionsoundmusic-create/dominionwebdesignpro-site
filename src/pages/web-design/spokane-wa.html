Spokane web design for two cities that share one name

Spokane Valley was unincorporated county land for more than a century. Voters narrowly approved becoming a city on May 21, 2002, after several failed attempts in the 1990s, and the new city officially began on March 31, 2003. The city says it is now home to more than 100,000 people, a city in its own right next door to Spokane.

That history matters for your site. Many people say "the Valley" and search that way. A roofer based in the Valley and a dentist downtown are both "Spokane" to someone in Seattle, but not to their neighbors. Say where you are by city name, and list the areas you cover by the names locals use.

Customers who drive in from the back country

Spokane draws people from a wide stretch of eastern Washington and north Idaho, especially for health care. The City of Spokane Valley calls the region the health care hub of the Inland Northwest and northern Idaho, with the largest concentration of medical services in four states.

They plan the trip

A family coming in from a small town for an appointment wants a place to eat, a pharmacy or a repair shop near the hospital, and they check before they leave home. Hours, parking and the nearest cross streets belong near the top of your page.

They come from Idaho too

Coeur d'Alene and Post Falls are across the state line in north Idaho. If you take customers or jobs from north Idaho, name those towns. If you work only in Washington, say that, so nobody calls from the wrong side of the border.

They call ahead

When the drive is long, people phone first. The number should dial with one tap from every page, and the site should say what to have ready: a part number, photos of the job, the size of the order.

Spokane website design that knows the districts

Spokane is a city of distinct districts, and naming yours helps a nearby customer recognize you as local. Browne's Addition, just west of downtown, is often called the city's oldest neighborhood and has a historic district on the National Register of Historic Places. The Garland District, north of downtown, is a commercial strip anchored by the 1940s-era Garland Theater. The South Perry District has a main street of cafes and shops and a weekly farmers market.

Kendall Yards shows how fast parts of the city change. Union Pacific used the land just north of downtown as a rail facility from 1914 to 1955, then it sat vacant for decades. The mixed-use neighborhood project got moving again in 2010 after stalling in the recession, and it now mixes shops, apartments and houses. A business that opened there recently has to introduce itself to customers who still remember an empty lot.

Downtown, Riverfront Park sits on the grounds of Expo '74, the world's fair that ran from May to November 1974 and drew more than 5 million visitors. The site was dedicated as Riverfront Park in 1978, and big events like Bloomsday and Hoopfest still bring crowds to the area. Those weekends bring crowds downtown, and a restaurant or shop nearby benefits from a site that shows event-day hours.
