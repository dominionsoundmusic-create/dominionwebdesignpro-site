Ask around town what Bakersfield runs on and you will hear two answers, oil and agriculture, often from the same person. Good Bakersfield website design starts there. A machine shop off Rosedale Highway may rebuild a pump one week and a harvester part the next, and a staffing firm may fill rig crews and packing shifts in the same month. The site has to speak to both kinds of buyer without confusing either.

Web design in Bakersfield for the two economies

The oil side

Kern County has long produced most of California's crude. The county's own lawsuit against the governor put its share at 71% of the state's oil and 78% of its natural gas, and The Guardian reported in 2021 that Kern produced about 70% of California's oil. In 2025 the state enacted SB 237, a law meant to speed new well permits in Kern County. Operators, service companies, inspectors and the shops that keep their equipment running all need sites that show capability and safety, not slogans.

The farm side

Kern's 2024 crop report totaled about $7.96 billion, down from a record $8.63 billion in 2023, the year Kern ranked as the top farm county in the country. Citrus led at $1.31 billion, then grapes at $1.18 billion and carrots at $1.01 billion, with almonds near $800 million. Growers, packers, haulers, labor contractors and irrigation crews make up a second business community that buys very differently from the first.

Trades that sell to both sides, and what their sites need

  • Welding and fabrication. Separate pages for field repair and shop work, the materials you weld and whether you send a rig out to the lease or the ranch.
  • Electrical and pump service. Well pumps, motors and panels for growers on one page, wellsite and facility work on another, each with your license details stated plainly.
  • Trucking and equipment hauling. Trailer types, lanes and what you can and cannot move, plus a short quote form that asks for pickup, drop and dates.
  • Staffing and safety training. Which roles you fill or which courses you teach, who they suit, and how a company or a worker gets started.

In every case the buyer is busy and reading on a phone. The first screen should say what you do and where, and the call button should work with one thumb.

A music town with honky-tonk roots

Bakersfield also gave its name to a sound. The Bakersfield sound grew up as a rougher answer to polished Nashville records, built on live musicianship, honky-tonk rhythm and a touch of rock and roll, and Buck Owens and Merle Haggard carried it onto the national charts. Owens opened the Crystal Palace in 1996 as a nightclub and a museum of country music; the family announced in August 2025 that it would close.

For restaurants, bars and event spaces that book live music, that history is a reason to keep a calendar page current. A show list that is three months stale tells people you may not be open at all.

Bakersfield website design that names the right side of town

The Census Bureau estimated Bakersfield at 417,468 people in July 2024, and the city is spread out. Oildale sits in North Bakersfield between Highway 99 and the Kern River. Seven Oaks and Stockdale are in the southwest. Rosedale Highway is one of the main east and west roads through the northwest, lined with shopping centers. Downtown has its own mix of offices, restaurants and older storefronts.

Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. A plumber in the southwest and a feed store in Oildale are reaching different neighbors, so each site should say where the business is and how far it travels, in the words residents use.
