East Texas web design for a region, not a single city

Texas Parks and Wildlife describes the Pineywoods as an ecoregion of about 40 counties along the state's eastern edge, running up against Louisiana and Arkansas, and most of its land is timberland. That shapes the economy as much as the scenery: sawmills and timber haulers, poultry and cattle, oil and gas service, and the welders, mechanics and contractors who keep all of it running.

It also shapes how people search. Someone in Ore City or Hallsville may drive half an hour to buy a part, see a specialist or get a truck fixed, so they look up the business before they leave home. If the only thing online is a Facebook page with old hours, they pick whoever looks open. That is the gap a plain, honest, fast website closes, and it is a gap almost every small town here still has.

What an East Texas business website has to do

Out here, a website rarely has to impress anyone. It has to answer four questions fast, on a phone with one bar of signal: what do you do, do you come out to my town, are you open, and how do I reach you. Plenty of small business sites around the region miss at least one of those.

  • Say the towns out loud. If you serve Big Sandy, Hawkins and Mineola, the site should say so, in words, on pages written for those places.
  • Put the phone number where a thumb can reach it. Tap to call at the top of every page, and the same number your truck and your Facebook page show.
  • Keep the hours honest. Seasonal hours, hunting season closures, festival weekends: if they change, the site should change with them.
  • Load quickly on a weak signal. Pages built light, photos sized properly, nothing that makes a visitor wait.
  • Belong to you. Your domain in your name, a one-time price, and no platform that takes the site away if you stop paying.

Metro or small town: two different jobs

In Tyler and Longview

You are competing. A search for a dentist, a roofer or a lawyer in either city brings back a full page of businesses, several of them with decent sites and a pile of reviews. What separates you there is specific copy: the neighborhoods you work in, the services you actually offer listed one by one, real photos of your crew and your shop, and a page for each service instead of one long list. That is where the Pro and Agency packages earn their price.

In the smaller towns

You are often the only one. In a town of a few thousand, the plumber or the cafe with a working website and a filled-in map listing is frequently the only result that looks open. The job is less about outranking anyone and more about simply existing online, accurately. A five-page Starter site does that well, and it is the first thing we would suggest for most single-location businesses in places like Gilmer, Gladewater or Big Sandy.

Many East Texas businesses live in both worlds at once: a contractor based in Kilgore who takes jobs in Longview, or a Gilmer shop that wants Tyler customers to make the drive. For them the answer is usually a solid main site plus city pages for the bigger towns they reach into, each one written for that town.
