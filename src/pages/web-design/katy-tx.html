Katy web design for a place that crosses county lines

The Katy Area Economic Development Council describes the area as three counties and one marketplace. A Katy mailing address does not mean a home or shop is inside the City of Katy, and much of the area east of the city sits in unincorporated land or in Houston's extraterritorial jurisdiction.

  • Cinco RanchA master-planned community and census-designated place in Fort Bend and Harris counties, with 16,899 residents in the 2020 census.
  • Cross Creek RanchA 3,200-acre community with a Fulshear address, a reminder that some Katy-area customers live in another city entirely.
  • ElysonNorth of I-10 along FM 529, beside an interchange on the Grand Parkway.
  • Old Town KatyThe historic downtown inside the city limits, where the Katy Rice Harvest Festival has been held each fall for decades.

Then come the roads everyone uses to give directions: I-10, the Grand Parkway, Fry Road and Mason Road. A homeowner in a newer section north of the freeway and one in an older section south of it may both say Katy, but they will not drive across the whole area for a haircut.

How to describe your service area when the address just says Katy

  1. Draw your real boundaryBefore anything is written, mark where you will actually go and where you will not. Honesty here saves wasted calls from across the county.
  2. Name communities, then roadsList the communities you serve, then the main roads that frame them. "South of I-10 between Fry and Mason" means something to a local.
  3. Give the busiest areas their own pageIf Cinco Ranch or Fulshear brings in a big share of your work, a page written for that place does more than a mention in a list.
  4. Keep the Google profile in stepYour Google Business Profile service area should match what the website says. Google weighs relevance, distance and prominence, and mixed signals help nobody.

Katy ISD households drive most of the demand

With more than 97,000 students and over 100 campuses and facilities in 2025 to 2026, Katy ISD shapes the local economy. Busy families keep air conditioning crews, pool and lawn services, roofers, pediatric dentists, music teachers, sports coaches and tutors on their phones.

Those parents usually search at odd hours, after practice or after the kids are asleep, and they compare a few businesses side by side. The site that states its service area, hours and prices or estimate process plainly is the one that gets the call in the morning.

Growth has also slowed a bit. Community Impact reported that the district's enrollment gains since 2024 to 2025 have been smaller than in earlier years. For a small business, that means competition for the same households is getting tighter, not looser, and clear pages matter more.

Old Town Katy and the businesses along the freeway

The rice festival is a good reminder of where Katy came from. It began in the late 1970s or early 1980s as a "Sellabration" honoring the rice farming that once ran the local economy, and it moved into downtown Katy as crowds grew.

Shops, restaurants and salons in Old Town compete on charm and foot traffic. Offices along I-10 and the Grand Parkway, such as law firms, insurance agencies and accountants, compete on credibility. Each needs a different first page: one shows the place and the hours, the other shows the people and their qualifications.
