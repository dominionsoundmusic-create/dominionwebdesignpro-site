What Houston's business makeup means for your website

Houston's big employers are famous: the energy companies along the Energy Corridor on Interstate 10, the hospitals of the Texas Medical Center, the Port of Houston and the plants along the Ship Channel. The small businesses that matter for web design are the ones that grow around them. Contractors who keep refinery-town homes running, clinics and therapists near the medical district, restaurants that feed shift workers, trucking and repair shops, cleaning crews, real estate agents, accountants.

The other thing that sets Houston apart is who the customers are. According to Census Bureau estimates for 2019 to 2023, 28.8 percent of Houston residents were born outside the United States, more than double the national share. Whole commercial districts reflect that. The stretch of Hillcroft Avenue and Harwin Drive in southwest Houston became the Mahatma Gandhi District in 2010, the first major commercial area in a U.S. city named for Gandhi, and farther west, Bellaire Boulevard runs through the city's Chinatown, an area packed with Asian restaurants, bakeries, markets and service businesses.

For your site, that means two practical decisions. Which neighborhoods and suburbs deserve their own page, because people search them by name? And does your audience need more than English to trust you? Both are worth deciding before the first page is written, not after launch.

What a Houston web design company should put on your site

Whoever you hire, a Houston small business site needs a few things that generic templates skip. Use this as a checklist when you compare quotes.

  • Your service area by name. Not "Greater Houston." The actual suburbs and neighborhoods, each with its own page if it brings in enough work.
  • A phone number that works with one tap. Most local searches happen on a phone, often from a truck or a parking lot.
  • Hours and a map. Houston traffic means people check where you are before they commit to the drive.
  • Real photos. Your crew, your shop, your work. Stock skylines do not sell a plumber.
  • Search basics done properly. Page titles, descriptions, a sitemap, and schema that tells Google your business type and service area.
  • Ownership in writing. Your domain in your name and your files yours to keep.
