First: what a city page actually is

When people talk about the SEO structure for a website that serves more than one town, they usually mean city pages. A city page is one page about one service, written for one town.

If you install flooring and you work in Kilgore, there is a page on your site about installing flooring in Kilgore. It mentions the town properly: the neighborhoods, the kind of houses, the sort of jobs you get there. A page that could be about anywhere reads like it was written for nobody.

The alternative most sites use is one page headed "Areas We Serve" with thirty town names underneath. It is about all of them and specific to none, and readers treat it as a list rather than an answer. Google's spam policies even single out blocks of text that list cities a page is trying to rank for.

People describe the same job differently

Two people want exactly the same thing from you. They type completely different things into Google. Say you remodel kitchens and bathrooms in Longview. All of these are the same customer:

  • kitchen remodel Longview
  • bathroom remodeling Longview
  • home renovation Longview
  • custom cabinets Longview
  • general contractor Longview
  • flooring installer Longview

One page cannot answer all six well. It ends up vague enough to cover them all, which means it is the best answer to none of them. Six pages can. Each one is specific, each one is about the thing that person actually typed, and each one still points at the same phone number.

So the number of pages is multiplication

Towns you serve, times the ways people describe what you do, equals pages.

TownsWays of describing itPagesWhat that covers
10110Your main service, in ten towns
10330Three services, across a real service area
12560Most of how customers describe you, everywhere you work

The table is arithmetic, not a menu. The research decides which combinations are worth a page. A town nobody searches from, paired with a phrase nobody types, does not earn one.

Which is what the two tiers actually buy

Pro: ten city pages

Your main service, in the ten towns that matter most, each town researched and then written from scratch. If you run a salon in Kilgore and almost everybody who walks in says "hair salon", ten pages is the right shape and more would be waste.

Agency: usually 30 to 60 pages

Keyword research across your whole service area comes first. Then a hand-written page for every town-and-service combination the research says is worth having, usually 30 to 60, delivered in waves with the first live inside two weeks. This suits a business where customers use genuinely different words: a contractor, a law office with several practice areas, a clinic with a long treatment list.

How this fits the SEO structure for your website

City pages are one layer of a simple structure. Google's SEO Starter Guide recommends organizing a site so people and search engines can see how pages relate, and using descriptive words in web addresses. In practice, for a local business, that looks like this:

  • Home page that says what you do and where.
  • Service pages, one per main service, explaining it properly.
  • City pages, one per town (or town and service) worth having, each linking back to the matching service.
  • Plain web addresses such as /flooring-kilgore-tx/, words separated by hyphens, which is what Google's URL guidance recommends.
  • One address per page, so the same content is not reachable at several URLs.
  • A sitemap listing every page, submitted to Google.

This site follows the same pattern: service pages, guides, and a city directory where each town page was researched before it was written.

What this does not promise

Building the pages is the part we control. Where they rank is not. Search results depend on your competition, your reviews, how well known your business is and a great many things nobody outside Google can see. Google itself says there is no way to pay for a better local ranking. Any agency that promises you a position is guessing, and charging you for the guess.
