The answer first

An SEO site description, properly called a meta description, is a short summary of one page that sits in the page's code. Search engines can show it as the gray text under your page's title in the results. Nobody reading your page sees it. It only appears out in the search results.

Three things are worth knowing before you spend an afternoon on it. It does not affect where you rank. Google often shows its own text instead. And it is still worth writing, one per page, for the pages that bring you customers, because when Google does use it, it is your sales line in front of someone who is deciding which business to call.

What Google actually does with it

Google's own help page on snippets, last updated April 20, 2026, says Google primarily uses the content on the page to decide what snippet to show, and sometimes uses the meta description if it gives a more accurate summary. Because the snippet is picked to match the search, one page can show different text for different searches.

How often does your text lose out? Ahrefs compared hand-written descriptions with Google's desktop results for 20,000 keywords in 2020 and found Google rewrote them 62.78% of the time. We know of no figure from Google itself, and the number will have moved since, but the lesson holds: you are making a suggestion, not a promise.

On ranking, Google has been plain for a long time. In a 2009 Search Central post, Google wrote that even though it sometimes uses the description meta tag for snippets, it does not use it in ranking. In a Reddit thread reported in June 2026, Google's John Mueller said that writing your own descriptions is not a requirement, carries no penalty, and is still worthwhile for individual pages you care about.

Notice what the better versions share. Each one names the service, the town and something that makes a person want to click, such as a price policy, opening days or what the page will explain. Only write things that are true for your business. If you do not post prices, do not say upfront prices.

How to write one in five minutes

Google's documentation asks for three things: a description that is different on every page, that summarizes what that particular page is about, and that reads like a sentence for a person rather than a string of keywords. For a local business page, that turns into a short routine.

  • Start with the service and the town. That is what the searcher typed, and Google cuts snippets off to fit the screen, so it belongs at the front.
  • Add one concrete reason to click. Your hours, a service area, how quotes work, or what the page will tell them.
  • Match the page. If the description promises prices and the page has none, the visitor leaves.
  • Keep it short enough to read on a phone. Google sets no hard limit. We keep ours to 160 characters or less so the main point survives.
  • Write the important pages first. Home, each main service and each town you serve. A privacy policy can wait.

The page title, the bold blue line above the description, matters more. Google's title guidance asks for a title that is unique, descriptive and concise, without repeated keywords. If you only have time for one, fix the titles first.

Where to change it, and how to see if it works

Every website builder has a place for it. In the Wix Editor, Wix's help center says to open the Pages and Menu panel, click the More Actions icon next to the page, choose SEO basics and edit the title tag and meta description. If you leave it blank, Wix fills it from the page's own description. Other builders usually keep the field in each page's settings under a heading like SEO. On a hand-coded site it is one line in the page's head: <meta name="description" content="...">.

To see what Google shows, search for the service and your town and look at your listing, or use Google Search Console, Google's own reporting tool for site owners. Its Performance report lists clicks, impressions and click-through rate, which Google defines as clicks divided by impressions, for each page. A page that shows up often but rarely gets clicked is a good place to rewrite the title and description first.
