What SEO web design actually is

SEO web design is building a website so search engines can find it, understand it and match it to the people searching for what you do. For a local business that mostly comes down to plain, careful work: each page about one thing, named clearly, written in the words customers use, fast on a phone, and connected to the other pages that relate to it.

None of it is secret. Google publishes its guidance in the Search Central documentation, and its own SEO Starter Guide says there are no secrets that will automatically rank a site first. Following best practices mainly makes a site easier for search engines to crawl and understand. That is the job we take on.

What follows is what we do on every site, why, and where the line is between what a web designer can control and what nobody can.

Speed and phones: the technical side of SEO for websites

Responsive web design for SEO is not optional anymore. Google indexes the mobile version of a page, so a site that hides content on phones hides it from search too.

Loading

Google's "good" mark for Largest Contentful Paint is 2.5 seconds or less. We serve photos in modern WebP format at several sizes, so a phone downloads a phone-sized image, and the main photo is loaded first.

Responding

Interaction to Next Paint should be under 200 milliseconds to count as good. Keeping scripts few and light is most of the battle, which is why our pages do not carry sliders and plugin stacks.

Holding still

Cumulative Layout Shift should stay below 0.1, meaning the page does not jump as it loads. Every image is given its width and height up front so the layout is reserved before the photo arrives.

Local SEO: pages, profile and proof

Most small business website SEO is local. Someone searches for a service plus a town, or for "near me", and Google picks results it thinks fit. Google's Business Profile help explains that local results are based mainly on three things: relevance, distance and prominence.

  • RelevanceHow well a listing matches the search. Complete, accurate business details and a website that clearly describes each service help here, and this is the part a website shapes most.
  • DistanceHow far the business is from the searcher or the place searched. No website changes where you are, which is why honest city pages explain how you serve a town rather than pretending to be in it.
  • ProminenceHow well known the business is, including links from other sites and the number of reviews and ratings. Reviews come from your customers over time; we show your real Google reviews on Pro and Agency sites and never write any.

Google also says you cannot pay or ask it for a better local ranking. That is worth remembering when someone offers to sell you one.

What SEO web design cannot promise

Here is the part many web design and SEO companies leave out. No one controls Google's results. Google's own page on hiring an SEO says to find someone else if they promise their changes will put you in first place, and it warns against anyone claiming a special relationship with Google or selling a "priority submit".

So we do not promise a position, a traffic number or a count of leads. What we can promise is the work itself: pages that are accurate, readable, fast, properly titled and marked up, linked together sensibly, and written for real searches your customers make. That gives Google the clearest possible picture of your business. Where it places you depends on competition, your reviews, your location and Google's systems, none of which a web designer owns.

A quick test for any SEO offer: if the pitch includes a ranking promise, a secret method, or a "special relationship" with Google, walk away. If it explains what will be built and how you can check it yourself, keep listening.

Keyword research and city pages, the slow way

The Agency package starts with keyword research across your whole service area: which towns and which services people actually search for, and how often. The research decides the page list. If it says your area is worth thirty pages, you get thirty; if it says twelve, you get twelve.

Each page is then written by hand for its town and service. That is deliberate. Google's guidance on helpful content asks whether a page was made for people or mainly to attract search visits, and a hundred near-identical pages with the town name swapped are the second kind. A page about a town should say something true about that town.

The Pro package includes ten city pages written the same way. How city pages work explains the method in more detail.
