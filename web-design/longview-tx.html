Longview is not a small town, and it does not search like one

Gregg County Judge Bill Stoudt put the local comparison plainly a few years back: whatever happens in Tyler and Smith County, Longview is about half of it — half the tax value, half the traffic. That sounds like a disadvantage until you look at the land. Gregg County is one of the smallest counties in Texas by area, roughly 271 square miles, and it holds more than 124,000 people. Longview itself runs around 82,000.

Dense and compact changes what a website has to do. Your customers are not spread across a hundred miles of country. They are close, they are searching on a phone, and the business that turns up first gets the call — whether that business is on High Street or over in Tyler, forty minutes west, spending real money to rank in your town.

That last part is the one most Longview owners miss. A Tyler firm can rank for a Longview search. A national outfit with no office within five hundred miles can too. Being here is only an advantage if the website says so clearly enough for Google to believe it.

There is new money coming into Gregg County

Longview's economy has never rested on one thing. Eastman Chemical and Dana have anchored manufacturing here for decades, and the East Texas oil field built the town before that. What has changed recently is the scale of what is arriving.

Old Navy and Gap built two one-million-square-foot warehouses in the area, turning Gregg County into a distribution point for goods shipping worldwide. Eastman has announced a second molecular recycling facility at its Longview site — described as the largest capital investment in the area's economic development history. East Texas Regional Airport has plans for new hangars, repaved runways and taxiway work that could total close to fifty million dollars in combined public and private money, and the I-20 East Texas Corridor project is set to widen key stretches of the interstate.

None of that is a website problem. But every one of those projects brings contractors, suppliers, trades, food, lodging and services — and those are businesses that get formed, get busy, and never quite get round to the website.

Who we build for in Longview

Most of the work here is for owner-operators — the person who answers the phone is the person doing the job. Trades and contractors, salons and barbers, medical and dental practices, restaurants, attorneys, real estate agents, and the shops along the older parts of town that have a Facebook page and nothing else.

The pattern is almost always one of three things:

  • No website at all. Everything runs through a Facebook page, which means Google has very little to show anyone searching for you by name, let alone by service.
  • A site built years ago that nobody has touched. Copyright line two or three years behind, phone number that no longer matches the one on your truck, hours that were right before you changed them.
  • A rented platform. The site lives on someone else's system, you do not own the domain, and if you stop paying it disappears.

All three are fixable, and none of them require you to learn anything.

Getting found in Longview specifically

Ranking in a town this size is a different job from ranking in Houston or Dallas. There are fewer competitors, which is good, and far fewer searches, which means every one counts. Three things do most of the work:

  • Your Google Business Profile, filled in properly and reviewed. For anything typed with "near me" attached, the map results sit above everything else and reviews largely decide the order. This is free and it is the single most neglected thing in East Texas.
  • Pages that name what you do and where you do it — not one page saying "serving East Texas", but real pages for the services people actually search, in the towns you actually cover. Longview, Kilgore, Gladewater, White Oak, Marshall, Henderson, Hallsville.
  • A site that loads fast on a phone. Most of your traffic is somebody standing in their driveway. If it takes five seconds they are gone before it finishes.

Every site we build includes the search setup. It is not an upsell.

Local resources worth knowing about

If you are starting or growing a business in Longview, these exist and cost nothing. The UT Tyler–Longview Small Business Development Center provides free consulting and training to businesses across Gregg, Harrison, Marion, Rusk, Panola and Upshur counties, funded in part through an agreement with the Small Business Administration. The Longview Economic Development Corporation handles site selection, business parks and workforce matters. The Longview Chamber of Commerce is the other one worth joining early — the local links and the referrals both matter more in a town this size than they would in a city.

We have no arrangement with any of them. They are simply the right first calls, and most owners here do not know they are free.
