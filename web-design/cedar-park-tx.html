A town of two hundred people is now the third largest in the metro

Cedar Park had around two hundred residents in 1960. It has more than eighty-two thousand now, making it the third largest city in the Austin metropolitan area. The local economic development effort reckons its work over the past twenty years has added close to nine and a half thousand jobs.

The town started on cedar posts and stone from the local quarry, and the commerce that grew along what became Bell Boulevard replaced that trade entirely. What is arriving now is different again: Firefly Aerospace and Hyliion are here, Texas Materials has consolidated its corporate headquarters in the city, and in early 2025 Cedar Park reported that ninety-five per cent of surveyed businesses were expanding.

A lot of money is about to be spent here

The development pipeline is unusually dense for a city this size — a $95 million Marriott hotel and convention centre due for completion by early 2027, a Residence Inn, a Tiger Woods-backed PopStroke entertainment complex reported for April 2026, a WorldSprings spa resort slated for May 2026, and a proposed Trader Joe's as part of a mixed-use project.

For an existing local business that cuts two ways. A convention centre and two hotels bring people who have never been to Cedar Park and know nothing about what is here. And every new entertainment complex and retail anchor brings competitors for the same attention.

Both point the same way: the businesses that are easy to find in 2027 will be the ones that set that up in 2026.

When ninety-five per cent of your neighbours are expanding

That figure is the most revealing thing about trading here, and it is a double-edged one. A business climate where almost everybody is growing means demand is real and money is moving. It also means everybody is hiring from the same workforce, bidding for the same premises, and chasing the same customers at the same time.

The businesses that struggle in a market like that are rarely the ones with a worse product. They are the ones who were harder to find while their competitor was expanding. Growth markets reward visibility more than settled ones do, because nobody has fixed habits yet.

Practical consequence: if you are planning to expand, the website work wants doing before the expansion, not after. Search takes months to move. A second location that opens with a site already ranking is in a different position from one that opens and then starts.

A workforce that will judge your site

Cedar Park's workforce draws on more than fifty universities, colleges and community colleges in the region, and the industry mix runs heavily to IT, advanced manufacturing and construction. Firefly Aerospace and Hyliion are here. Fuse Workspace has opened a coworking location in the city.

That matters for an unglamorous reason. A customer base weighted toward technical and professional work has a low tolerance for a website that looks abandoned. A copyright line three years out of date, hours that are wrong, a contact form that bounces — those read as carelessness to that audience in a way they might not elsewhere.

It does not mean you need something elaborate. It means the basics have to be right and current, which is a much cheaper problem to solve than most people assume.
