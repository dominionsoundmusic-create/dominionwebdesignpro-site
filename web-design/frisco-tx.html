In Frisco, almost nobody has word of mouth yet

Frisco has gone from about thirty-four thousand residents to roughly two hundred and forty-three thousand in twenty-five years. That is not growth, it is a different town every few years, and it changes what a local business can rely on.

In a settled town, a good trade builds a reputation over decades and referrals do most of the work. Here, a large share of your potential customers moved in recently, know almost nobody, and have no neighbour to ask. What they have instead is a phone.

That is the whole argument for a website in this city, and it is a stronger one than in most places. In Frisco, search is not supplementing word of mouth. For a great many of your customers it is replacing it, because they do not have any.

And more people are coming

Universal Kids Resort, a theme park for families with young children reported at around $550 million, is set to open in Frisco in 2026. Collin County as a whole has seen its GDP grow nearly fivefold since 2000 and is projected to account for roughly a tenth of the entire Texas economy by 2050 — an economy larger by then than several US states.

For a restaurant, a salon, a trades business or a shop, that means two waves at once: more residents who do not know you, and visitors who have never been here. Both find things the same way.

A new business has nothing to point at, and that is the problem to solve

If you opened here in the last couple of years you are in the hardest position a local business can be in. No reviews yet. No years-in-business line to lean on. No customer who can vouch for you to the person reading. Everything a settled competitor takes for granted, you have to build from nothing while paying rent.

What a website can do about that is narrower than most agencies imply, and worth being precise about. It cannot manufacture a reputation. What it can do is show the work — photographs of finished jobs, plainly described services, real prices where you are willing to give them, and a face with a name attached. In the absence of a reputation, evidence is what people accept instead.

Questions worth asking

Does it matter that you are not in Frisco?

For building a website, no. The work is the same from here. Where location would matter is a full brand programme with people in the room for weeks, which is a different service.

My business is new here. What should the site say?

Who you are, what you do, where you cover, and any proof you have — photographs of finished work count for more than adjectives. In a town this new, most people reading it have no way to check with a neighbour.

Do I own the website?

Yes. The domain is registered in your name and the files are yours. If you move to another company later, you take the site with you.

How long does it take?

A five page site is usually ready to look at within a few days of getting your photos and details. What slows it down is almost never the building — it is waiting on photos.
