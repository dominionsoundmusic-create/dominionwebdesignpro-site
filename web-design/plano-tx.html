You are a small business being quoted corporate prices

Plano has more than three hundred thousand residents and hosts Toyota North America, JPMorgan Chase and Hewlett Packard Enterprise, with AT&T's new global headquarters and the Texas Research Quarter still to come. Legacy West turned a stretch of the city into walkable corporate urbanism and changed how the whole region is perceived.

That is excellent for Plano and awkward for a small business in it. Agencies here price against clients who have marketing departments and budgets to match. When a two-person firm calls the same agency, the quote does not shrink to fit — it arrives calibrated to a market where the neighbouring customer is a Fortune 500 subsidiary.

It shows up in the numbers. Advertisers bidding on "web design plano tx" pay around $26 a click. That is the cost of one visitor, before anybody has designed anything, and it sits inside every quote in this city.

We are not in Plano, and it does not matter

Dominion Web Design Pro is in East Texas. We are saying that early because it is true and because it changes nothing about the work.

A website is not poured concrete. Nobody has to be standing in your city to build one, and a company that leans hard on being local is usually selling you the office rather than the site. What a good website actually needs is somebody who knows how to build one and somebody who knows the business. We bring the first. What is left is price, ownership, and whether you see the thing before you pay for it.

You will own it

Ask any web company one question before you sign: if I stop paying you, what do I keep? A surprising number of arrangements answer that with "nothing" — the site lives on the agency's platform and the domain sits in their account.

Every site we build belongs to the client. The domain is registered in your name, the files are yours, and if you decide next year that you would rather have a Plano agency run it, you take the site with you and we help with the handover.

That also changes the incentive. A company that owns your website has a reason to keep you dependent. A company that sold you one has to earn the next thing on merit.

What a Plano small business actually needs

Not what the corporate campuses need. A five page site covers most of it: a home page that says what you do and where, a page per service, an about page with real photographs of real people, a contact page that works on a phone, and whatever proof you have — jobs, reviews, credentials.

What we would tell you not to buy, at least at first, is a two hundred page location build. We know because we did it on our own sites and published what happened: one has 348 generated city pages and Google indexed two of them. If somebody includes hundreds of location pages in your quote, ask how many were indexed on the last site they built.

And one thing we do not do, said plainly: if you want a full brand programme with workshops and people in the room for weeks, that is not this. Plano has firms who do it properly and charge accordingly. We build websites.
