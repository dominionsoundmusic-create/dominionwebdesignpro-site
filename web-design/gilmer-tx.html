One week a year, Gilmer is not a small town

Gilmer runs to about five thousand people most of the year. Then the third weekend of October arrives and the East Texas Yamboree turns the square into a carnival, and the town takes in something north of a hundred thousand visitors over four days.

Think about that as a business owner rather than as a local. For one week, twenty times your town's population is walking around looking for somewhere to eat, somewhere to stay, something to buy, and somebody who can fix the thing that just broke. Almost none of them know your business exists. Every single one of them has a phone.

If someone standing on the courthouse square in October searches for what you sell and you do not come up, that is not a small miss. That is the busiest week of your year going to whoever did show up.

The Yamboree has run since 1935, when the state asked counties to hold festivals for the Texas Centennial and Upshur County chose the sweet potato — the crop had just come back after a weevil quarantine shut the shipping down for years. It has only been called off twice, for the Second World War and for the pandemic. It is one of the oldest continuously running festivals in Texas, and it is the single largest commercial event that happens to a Gilmer business all year.

Most towns have nothing like it. Planning your website around it is a genuinely local advantage that a web company in Dallas would never think to mention.

The rest of the year, you are competing with Longview and Tyler

Gilmer sits on Highway 271, twenty-two miles southeast of nothing much and about the same northwest of Longview, with Tyler thirty-five miles down the road. That is close enough that your customers will drive, and close enough that Longview and Tyler businesses show up in Gilmer searches without trying.

Upshur County's economy is not one thing either. Timber and sawmills, cattle, poultry, hay, oil and gas, food processing and metal fabrication all sit alongside the retail on the square. It has been that way a long while — Gilmer was a cotton-ginning town first, with six gins running at one point and one still going into the 1950s, before sweet potatoes and then oil came through.

What that mix means for a website is simple enough: the businesses here serve a spread-out rural county, not a dense neighbourhood. People search before they drive. If they cannot find you, they do not drive.

Who we build for around Gilmer

The pattern is nearly always one of three:

All three are fixable, and none of them require you to learn anything or sit through a training session.

Getting found in a town this size

Ranking in Gilmer is a different job from ranking in Houston. There is far less competition, which is good, and far fewer searches, which means each one matters more. Three things do most of the work:

  • Your Google Business Profile, filled in and reviewed. For anything typed with "near me" attached, the map results sit above everything else, and reviews largely decide the order. It is free, and in a town this size a handful of reviews can put you at the top. Almost nobody here bothers.
  • Pages naming what you do and where. Gilmer, Big Sandy, Ore City, Union Grove, Harmony, Pittsburg, Longview, Gladewater — the places you would actually drive to.
  • Loading fast on a phone. Most of your traffic is somebody standing in a parking lot. Five seconds and they are gone.

The search setup is included in every build. It is not an upsell.

Questions people here ask

Is a website really worth it for a business this small?

Honestly, sometimes not. If you are booked solid off word of mouth and turning work away, it is not your problem yet. It becomes your problem when the referrals slow down — and by then a site takes months to start earning, so the time to build one is while you are busy, not after.

I do not pay until I have seen it?

Correct. The site gets built before there is an invoice. It is the only fair way to have this conversation with somebody who has been sold a website before and got very little back.

How long does it take?

A five page site is usually ready to look at within a few days of us having your photos and details. What slows it down is nearly always waiting on photos.

Can you promise I will come up first on Google?

No, and nobody honest will. We build the foundation properly — page titles, service and location pages, schema, fast mobile loading, search console. Where you land and how fast is not something anyone can guarantee.

Local things worth knowing

If you are starting a business in Upshur County, the County Clerk's office at 100 West Tyler Street in Gilmer handles assumed name certificates — the DBA filing you need if you trade under anything other than your own legal name. For a sole proprietor it runs somewhere around sixteen dollars, so it is not the expensive part of starting up, it is just the part people forget.

The UT Tyler–Longview Small Business Development Center provides free consulting and training to businesses across Upshur County along with Gregg, Harrison, Marion, Rusk and Panola, funded in part through an agreement with the Small Business Administration. Free, and most owners around here have never heard of it. The Historic Upshur Museum on the square, in the old 1925 post office, is also worth an hour if you ever need photographs or background for your own About page — there are more than fifty historical markers in and around the county.
