Your customer decided before they got in the car

New Braunfels sits at the confluence of the Comal and Guadalupe rivers and has been on lists of the fastest-growing communities in the nation for more than a decade — roughly thirty-five thousand people in 2000, something like a hundred and ten thousand now. It runs on water recreation, German heritage, festivals and the visitors all three bring.

Which means a very large share of the money spent here is spent by people who chose where they were going before they arrived. They booked the lodging from home. They picked the restaurant on the drive down. They decided which outfitter, which shop, which tour, sitting somewhere else entirely with a phone.

A business serving that trade is not competing on the street. It is competing in a search result three days earlier, against every other option the visitor found. If you are not in that comparison, the quality of what you offer never gets tested.

What a tourism-facing site has to answer

Visitors ask a narrow set of questions and abandon a site that will not answer them. What does it cost. What are the hours, today, not in general. Do I need to book. Where do I park. How long does it take. Is it suitable for children. What happens if it rains.

Most local sites answer none of these and lead with a paragraph about the owner's passion instead. The passion matters, but it belongs further down. A visitor with four options open in four tabs is eliminating on missing information, and they are doing it quickly.

Seasonal hours deserve their own warning. Nothing costs a tourism business more than a website showing last summer's schedule. If your hours change through the year, they need to be somewhere you can update in a minute — and you need to actually do it.

The town has been managing growth for a long time

There is a detail that says a lot about how seriously this is taken locally. In 1985 New Braunfels drew all of its water from the Edwards Aquifer. Today that source supplies something like eighteen per cent, after a deliberate decade-long effort to diversify supply.

That is a town that plans rather than reacts, and it is worth matching that as a business. The equivalent for you is unglamorous: current hours, working booking links, real photographs taken this year, a phone number somebody answers, and reviews you have actually asked for.
