Growing faster than the pipes

Kyle has repeatedly turned up on lists of the fastest-growing communities in America, and there is a detail that captures what that actually means better than any population figure. The city maxed out its allowable draw from the Edwards Aquifer and had to strike a deal with San Marcos to buy some of its neighbor's water supply.

That is what growth looks like from the inside — not a statistic, but a town arriving at limits nobody planned for and negotiating its way past them. The same thing happens commercially. New residential developments fill up faster than the businesses serving them can establish, and national chains along the I-35 corridor arrive with marketing budgets that a local independent cannot match.

The advantage a local business has in that situation is not price and it is not advertising. It is being the one that comes up when somebody new in town searches for what they need — and that is a matter of having a site worth ranking, not of outspending anybody.

The whole corridor is being built out at once

The stretch of Interstate 35 between Austin and San Antonio has become one of the fastest-changing economic corridors in the country. Land that sat empty is now retail centers, hospitality, housing and industrial space, running more or less continuously through Kyle, San Marcos, New Braunfels and beyond.

For an established Kyle business that is a mixed blessing. More people, and more competitors arriving with professional marketing already in place. The businesses that hold their ground through that are the ones whose customers can find them without being reminded — which is a search problem and a reviews problem more than an advertising one.

Hays County has more free business support than most owners use

Worth knowing before you spend anything with anybody. The Governor's Small Business Summit has been hosted for Hays County in partnership with the Kyle Area Chamber of Commerce and the chambers in Dripping Springs, Buda, San Marcos and Wimberley, alongside the Texas Workforce Commission, with Kyle Economic Development among the sponsors.

Events like that are free or close to it, they are aimed squarely at businesses your size, and most owners we speak to have never attended one. We have no involvement in any of it and get nothing from mentioning it. It is simply there, and the chamber is the first call rather than the last.

Keeping a small-town feel while doubling in size

Kyle, Buda and San Marcos have all spent years trying to hold onto a sense of place while absorbing enormous growth — welcoming the people leaving Austin without becoming an extension of it. Local officials talk about this openly, and it shapes what customers here respond to.

For a business it means something practical about how your website should sound. A site written in the voice of a national chain works against you in a town that is actively resisting becoming one. Plain language, real photographs of the actual place, the owner's name, and no stock imagery of people who do not work there — that is what reads as local, and it costs nothing extra.

What we would tell you not to buy is a two hundred page location build. We did exactly that on our own sites — 348 generated pages, two indexed by Google.
