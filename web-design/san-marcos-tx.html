Three customer bases, and none of them stay

San Marcos is an unusual commercial town because almost nobody buying from you lives there permanently. There is the university population, which turns over continuously. There are river visitors, who come for a day. And there is Interstate 35 traffic between Austin and San Antonio, which stops only if something makes it worth stopping.

Every one of those groups shares a trait: they have no local knowledge and no neighbor to ask. A student in their first month, a family driving down for the afternoon, a traveller deciding whether to pull off the highway — all of them are searching, and whatever they find is what they choose.

That makes San Marcos one of the few towns where a website is not a supplement to word of mouth. There is not enough continuity in the population for word of mouth to accumulate in the ordinary way.

The corridor is filling in around you

The I-35 stretch between Austin and San Antonio has become one of the most rapidly developing economic corridors in the United States — former empty land turned into retail centers, hospitality and housing, running continuously through Kyle, San Marcos and New Braunfels. A large Buc-ee's travel center development has been part of that in San Marcos.

National operators arriving on a highway corridor come with signage, budgets and brand recognition. A local business cannot match that on spend. What it can do is be the result that comes up when somebody searches for the specific thing it does — which costs a fraction as much and works better for anyone who was looking for something particular rather than something convenient.

Every September, your customers are replaced

The university calendar is the single most useful thing to plan a small business's marketing around here, and most owners treat it as weather rather than as a schedule. A large cohort arrives each autumn knowing nothing about the town — where to eat, who cuts hair, who fixes a laptop, where to get a tire changed.

They will search for all of it in their first fortnight and then mostly keep whatever they found. Being findable in August and September is therefore worth more than being findable in February, and a site that ranks by mid-summer is capturing a year's worth of habit formation.

The practical version: get the site and the Google profile right in spring, not in October.
