In this town, the website is the shop window

The Texas Legislature officially named Dripping Springs the Wedding Capital of Texas in 2015, and the numbers behind that are remarkable. A former ranching town twenty-odd miles west of Austin has gone from a single wedding venue to more than thirty-five inside fifteen square miles, hosting somewhere around a thousand weddings a year, with more than twenty-five wineries, breweries and distilleries alongside them.

Which makes this the one town on our list where a website is not a supporting asset. It is the storefront. Nobody walks past a venue down a Hill Country ranch road and wanders in. Every single booking begins with a couple in Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth or further afield, sitting with a laptop, comparing places they have never seen.

They will judge you almost entirely on photographs and on whether the practical questions are answered. If your site is slow, thin on pictures, or vague about capacity and pricing, you are eliminated before anyone speaks to you — and you will never know it happened.

What a venue or vendor site has to do

Photographs, and a lot of them. More than feels reasonable. A couple wants to see the ceremony space, the reception space, the getting-ready rooms, the light at different times of day, and what it looks like in rain. This is the one industry where a gallery genuinely is the product.

Capacity and pricing, plainly stated. Vendors resist this and it costs them. A couple with a hundred and forty guests and a set budget is filtering ruthlessly, and a site that will not say either number gets skipped for one that will. Publishing a starting figure loses you the enquiries you would have lost anyway and wins you the ones who were qualified.

Speed on a phone. Much of this browsing happens in the evening on a sofa. A gallery-heavy site that takes six seconds is a gallery nobody sees.

An enquiry form that works and is answered. In a market where florists handle a hundred and eighty weddings a year and planners run several parties a weekend, response time is a competitive weapon. If your form is quietly failing, you will not find out from the couples who did not hear back.

The vendors, not just the venues

The venues get the attention, but the town runs on everybody else: florists, photographers, planners, DJs, caterers, hair and make-up, transport, rentals, officiants. Many are one or two people working weekends at capacity, and most of them are found through a couple typing "Dripping Springs florist" or through a venue's recommended-vendor list.

Both routes need the same thing — a real site with real work on it. A social profile is not enough, because a couple planning from four hundred miles away wants to see a body of work in one place, not scroll a feed.

And beyond weddings, this is a destination town in its own right: tasting rooms, distilleries, restaurants, boutiques and lodging, all serving visitors who decided where to go before they got in the car.
