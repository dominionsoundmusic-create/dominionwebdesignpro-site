The short answer

Why the range is so wide

Because "website" describes three completely different purchases, and quotes rarely say which one you are being sold.

You build it yourself

Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy and similar run roughly $15 to $50 a month for a standard site. There is no build fee, and the monthly payment never stops. Published 2026 breakdowns put DIY builders at about $17 to $45 a month, with ecommerce plans higher.

The real cost here is not money, it is your evenings. You become the designer, the copywriter and the support desk. That is a fair trade if you have the time and only need something simple. It is a bad trade if your time is worth more in the business.

You hire a freelancer

The consistent figure across several 2026 analyses is $1,500 to $8,000 for a custom small business site, at roughly $50 to $150 an hour. One breakdown puts a basic five to eight page site at $2,500 to $7,000 from a freelancer. Another has template-based brochure sites clustering at $3,000 to $8,000.

You get a real person making decisions. What varies enormously is what happens after launch, because that depends entirely on whether that one person is still available.

You hire an agency

Boutique agencies generally start around $6,000 and run to $35,000 and beyond. The same basic five to eight page site that a freelancer quotes at $2,500 to $7,000 comes in at $5,000 to $15,000 from an agency, and one analysis found a freelancer typically charges 40 to 55 per cent of an agency price for identical scope.

The gap is not craftsmanship. It is that agencies carry project managers, designers, account staff and offices, which one 2026 pricing study describes as a two to three times markup on the same work.

The costs almost nobody quotes you

This is where budgets actually break, and it is worth reading before you accept any quote.

Ongoing costs for a small business site run somewhere between $1,000 and $6,000 a year once everything is counted. Maintenance alone is typically $50 to $200 a month with a freelancer and $150 to $500 a month with an agency. Domain registration is $10 to $20 a year. Hosting ranges from a couple of dollars a month to well over a hundred. Premium plugins on a WordPress build add $100 to $400 a year.

Then there are the things frequently excluded from the build quote entirely. Copywriting for a five page site runs $500 to $2,500 if you are not writing it yourself. Stock photography is $15 to $50 an image, which on a ten page site with three images a page adds $450 to $1,500. A full SEO setup with keyword research and technical work adds $500 to $2,000 on top.

Ask thisWhat is the total cost in year one, and what is it in year two?

A $3,000 build with $300 a month of care is a $6,600 first year and a $3,600 second year. A $6,000 build with $49 a month is $6,588 and then $588. The build price on its own tells you very little.

Where agency money actually goes

Some of it goes into the site. A good deal of it goes into finding you.

We looked up what advertisers currently pay for a single click on these searches. "Web design for small business" costs about $40.87 a click. "Web designers near me" is $35.40. "Web design services" is $36.69. That is the price of one visitor, not one customer — and if one visitor in fifty becomes a client, the acquisition cost runs into four figures before anybody has designed anything.

That cost lives inside every quote in this market. It is not dishonest, it is arithmetic. But it does mean a meaningful share of what you pay for a website is paying for the marketing that reached you.

How to compare quotes without getting lost

Five questions. They take a minute each and they separate real quotes from vague ones.

1. If I stop paying you, what do I keep?

The most important question in this industry and the one least often asked. A surprising number of arrangements answer it with "nothing" — the site lives on the company's platform and the domain is in their account. Ours answers it with "everything": the domain is registered in your name and the files are yours.

2. What is the total in year one and in year two?

Get both numbers in writing. Recurring cost, not build cost, is what determines whether a website was a good purchase three years later.

3. Is copywriting included, or am I writing it?

A great many quotes exclude it and the project stalls for months while the owner tries to write their own about page. Find out before you sign, not after.

4. Who writes the content for my services?

If the answer involves a large number of automatically generated location pages, ask how many were indexed on the last site they built. We know why that matters because we did it on our own sites: one has 348 generated city pages and Google indexed two of them.

5. Can I see it before I pay?

Most will say no, because building on spec costs them money. It is still worth asking, because the answer tells you how confident they are.

So what should you actually spend?

A Clutch survey found that 61 per cent of small business buyers spent under $10,000 on their most recent website project, down from 68 per cent in 2023 — the market is drifting upward, not because sites got better but because more is being bundled in.

Our honest view: for a local business with fewer than about twenty pages and no ecommerce, anything above roughly $3,000 needs a specific justification. Ask what the extra buys. Sometimes the answer is good — genuine custom design, real photography, a complicated booking integration. Sometimes it is an account manager and an office.

And if a quote is far below the market, including ours, ask the same question in reverse: what is missing? We have listed what is missing above. Anyone who cannot do the same is worth a second look.
